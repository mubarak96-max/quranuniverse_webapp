Astaghfirullah (*astaghfiru Allah*, أستغفر الله) means "I seek forgiveness from God." Muslims say it after a mistake or sin, three times after every prayer, and many times a day as remembrance. The Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) said he sought God's forgiveness more than seventy times a day (Sahih al-Bukhari 6307).

The practice of saying it is called **istighfar** (*istighfar*, استغفار), seeking forgiveness. It rests on one of the most hopeful ideas in the Quran: no one is beyond God's forgiveness while they still have breath to ask. This guide covers the meaning, the Arabic, the Quranic promises, the master dua of forgiveness, and how the phrase is used and misused in daily speech.

## Key takeaways

- Astaghfirullah means "I seek forgiveness from God," from the root *gh-f-r*, which carries the idea of covering and protecting.
- In the Quran, Prophet Noah promised his people that seeking forgiveness would bring rain, wealth, children, gardens and rivers (Quran 71:10-12).
- The Prophet called one dua *Sayyid al-Istighfar*, "the master of seeking forgiveness," and promised Paradise to one who says it with firm faith and dies that day or night (Sahih al-Bukhari 6306).
- The Prophet sought forgiveness more than 70 times a day (Sahih al-Bukhari 6307), and in another report 100 times a day (Sahih Muslim 2702).
- After finishing each prayer, the Prophet said astaghfirullah three times (Sahih Muslim 591).

## What does astaghfirullah mean?

The phrase has two parts. *Astaghfiru* is a verb meaning "I seek forgiveness," and *Allah* is the Arabic word for God. The verb is built on the Arabic pattern that expresses asking for something, so *astaghfiru* means more than "forgive me." It means "I am actively asking for forgiveness."

The root *gh-f-r* originally means to cover and to shield. An Arabic word for a soldier's helmet, *mighfar*, comes from the same root. Forgiveness in this sense is God covering a person's sin so it is not exposed, and shielding them from its consequences. Two of God's names come from this root: *al-Ghaffar*, "the Constant Forgiver," and *al-Ghafur*, "the All-Forgiving." You can read about them on the pages for [al-Ghaffar](/names/al-ghaffar) and [al-Ghafur](/names/al-ghafur).

## Astaghfirullah in Arabic and how to spell it

In Arabic, astaghfirullah is written أستغفر الله. A longer form, used by the Prophet himself in Sahih al-Bukhari 6307, adds a promise to repent: أستغفر الله وأتوب إليه (*astaghfirullaha wa atubu ilayh*), "I seek God's forgiveness and turn to Him in repentance."

| Spelling | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Astaghfirullah | The most common English spelling |
| Astagfirullah | Drops the "h" that marks the Arabic letter *ghayn* |
| Astaghfir Allah | Separates the verb from God's name |
| Astaghfirullah al-Azim | Adds "the Magnificent," a common extended form |
| Estağfurullah | Turkish spelling, also used as a polite "not at all" |

A frequent misspelling is "astaghfirallah." The vowel before *Allah* is a "u" in the standard pronunciation, so "astaghfirullah" is the better form.

## Where does astaghfirullah come from? Quran 71:10-12

The Quran calls on people to seek God's forgiveness again and again. One of the best-known passages comes from Surah Nuh, where Prophet Noah describes how he appealed to his people:

> And said, 'Ask forgiveness of your Lord. Indeed, He is ever a Perpetual Forgiver. He will send [rain from] the sky upon you in [continuing] showers And give you increase in wealth and children and provide for you gardens and provide for you rivers.
>
> — [Quran 71:10–12](/surah/nuh/10)

This is why many Muslims turn to istighfar when facing drought, debt or difficulty having children. Seeking forgiveness is presented not only as spiritual cleansing but as a cause of blessing in this world.

Other verses carry the same message. Prophet Hud told his people that seeking forgiveness would "increase you in strength [added] to your strength" ([11:52](/surah/hud/52)). And the Quran gives this open invitation:

> And whoever does a wrong or wrongs himself but then seeks forgiveness of Allah will find Allah Forgiving and Merciful.
>
> — [Quran 4:110](/surah/an-nisa/110)

## Istighfar benefits in the Quran and hadith

People often search for the benefits of istighfar. These are the ones grounded in the Quran and authentic hadith.

| Benefit | Source |
| --- | --- |
| Forgiveness and mercy from God | Quran 4:110 |
| Rain, wealth, children, gardens and rivers | Quran 71:10-12 |
| Good provision and enjoyment of life "for a specified term" | Quran 11:3 |
| Added strength | Quran 11:52 |
| Protection from collective punishment | Quran 8:33 |
| Praise from God for those who seek forgiveness before dawn | Quran 3:17 |
| Paradise for one who says Sayyid al-Istighfar with certainty and dies that day or night | Sahih al-Bukhari 6306 |

A popular hadith says that whoever keeps seeking forgiveness will be given a way out of every distress, relief from every worry, and provision from where he does not expect. It is recorded in Sunan Abi Dawud 1518, but al-Albani graded it weak. Its meaning is close to Quran [65:2-3](/surah/at-talaq/2), which makes a similar promise to those who fear God.

Sahih Muslim 2749 adds a striking perspective: the Prophet said that if people did not sin, God would replace them with people who would sin, seek His forgiveness, and be forgiven. The point is not to encourage sin but to show that God loves to forgive those who turn back to Him.

## Sayyid al-Istighfar: the master dua of forgiveness

The Prophet named one supplication *Sayyid al-Istighfar*, "the master of seeking forgiveness" (Sahih al-Bukhari 6306). Muslims say it in the morning and evening.

**Arabic:** اللَّهُمَّ أَنْتَ رَبِّي، لاَ إِلَهَ إِلاَّ أَنْتَ، خَلَقْتَنِي وَأَنَا عَبْدُكَ، وَأَنَا عَلَى عَهْدِكَ وَوَعْدِكَ مَا اسْتَطَعْتُ، أَعُوذُ بِكَ مِنْ شَرِّ مَا صَنَعْتُ، أَبُوءُ لَكَ بِنِعْمَتِكَ عَلَىَّ وَأَبُوءُ لَكَ بِذَنْبِي، فَاغْفِرْ لِي، فَإِنَّهُ لاَ يَغْفِرُ الذُّنُوبَ إِلاَّ أَنْتَ

**Transliteration:** Allahumma anta Rabbi, la ilaha illa anta, khalaqtani wa ana 'abduka, wa ana 'ala 'ahdika wa wa'dika mastata'tu, a'udhu bika min sharri ma sana'tu, abu'u laka bini'matika 'alayya, wa abu'u laka bidhanbi, faghfir li, fa innahu la yaghfirudh-dhunuba illa anta.

**Translation:**

> O Allah, You are my Lord, there is none worthy of worship except You. You have created me, and I am Your servant, and I am faithful to Your covenant and promise as much as I can. I seek refuge in You from the evil of what I have done. I acknowledge Your blessings upon me, and I admit my sins. So forgive me, for none forgives sins except You.
>
> — Sahih al-Bukhari 6306

The Prophet added: "If somebody recites it during the day with firm faith in it, and dies on the same day before the evening, he will be from the people of Paradise; and if somebody recites it at night with firm faith in it, and dies before the morning, he will be from the people of Paradise."

The dua moves through a complete act of repentance: it affirms God as Lord, renews a promise, seeks protection, acknowledges His favors, confesses one's sins and asks forgiveness from the only One who can grant it. You will find it alongside other morning and evening supplications in our guide to [daily duas](/learn/daily-duas).

## The Prophet's 70 to 100 times a day

Abu Hurayrah reported that the Prophet said: "By Allah! I ask for forgiveness from Allah and turn to Him in repentance more than seventy times a day" (Sahih al-Bukhari 6307). Another companion, al-Agharr al-Muzani, reported him saying, "There is (at times) some sort of shade upon my heart, and I seek forgiveness from Allah a hundred times a day" (Sahih Muslim 2702).

Muslims believe prophets were protected from major sins, so why did he seek forgiveness so often? Scholars give several answers. His istighfar expressed humility and gratitude, since no one can fully thank God as He deserves. It covered any moment of distraction from God, which the "shade upon my heart" may describe. And it set an example for his followers. The Quran itself told him, near the end of his mission, to "exalt [Him] with praise of your Lord and ask forgiveness of Him" ([110:3](/surah/an-nasr/3)).

## When do Muslims say astaghfirullah?

- **Right after prayer.** Thawban reported that when the Prophet finished his prayer, he said astaghfirullah three times, then "O Allah, You are Peace, and from You comes peace; blessed are You, O Possessor of Glory and Honour" (Sahih Muslim 591).
- **After a sin or mistake.** The Quran praises those who, "when they commit an immorality or wrong themselves... remember Allah and seek forgiveness for their sins" ([3:135](/surah/ali-imran/135)).
- **Before dawn.** The Quran praises "those who seek forgiveness before dawn" ([3:17](/surah/ali-imran/17)), which is why istighfar is closely tied to the night prayer.
- **In the morning and evening**, with Sayyid al-Istighfar.
- **Throughout the day**, as simple remembrance.

## Istighfar vs tawbah

Istighfar is often paired with **tawbah** (*tawbah*, توبة), repentance. They overlap but are not identical.

| Feature | Istighfar | Tawbah |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meaning | Asking God to forgive | Turning back to God |
| Main form | A request made with the tongue and heart | A change of direction in life |
| What it involves | Saying astaghfirullah or a dua of forgiveness | Regret, stopping the sin, resolving not to return, and righting wrongs done to others |
| Example phrase | Astaghfirullah | Wa atubu ilayh, "and I turn to Him" |
| Quran pairing | "Seek forgiveness of your Lord and repent to Him" (11:3) | Same verse |

The verse about sin quoted in the list above adds a condition: those it praises "do not persist in what they have done while they know." Saying astaghfirullah while intending to repeat the sin misses its heart. Our guide [does God forgive all sins?](/learn/does-god-forgive-all-sins) explains repentance in depth.

## Astaghfirullah as an exclamation

In everyday speech, many Muslims say "astaghfirullah!" when they hear something shocking, offensive or inappropriate, much as an English speaker might say "God forbid" or "good grief."

This usage has a reasonable basis, since the speaker is distancing themselves from wrongdoing and turning to God. Problems arise when the phrase loses its meaning:

- **Using it to judge others.** Saying it loudly at someone else's mistake can sound like condemnation rather than humility. Istighfar is first about one's own sins.
- **Using it as a joke or sarcasm.** Turning a request for God's forgiveness into a punchline empties it.
- **Using it as filler.** Said a hundred times without thought, it becomes a habit of the tongue rather than the heart.

## How do you reply to astaghfirullah?

There is no fixed reply. After someone admits a mistake, "May God forgive us all" or "Ameen" is kind. As an exclamation, it needs no reply.

## Common mistakes with astaghfirullah

- **Spelling it "astaghfirallah."** The standard form ends in *-ullah*.
- **Saying it while planning to repeat the sin**, against the condition described above.
- **Despairing of forgiveness.** The Quran says, "do not despair of the mercy of Allah. Indeed, Allah forgives all sins" ([39:53](/surah/az-zumar/53)).

## Frequently asked questions

### What does astaghfirullah mean?

Astaghfirullah means "I seek forgiveness from God." It comes from the Arabic root *gh-f-r*, meaning to cover and protect, so forgiveness is God covering a sin and shielding a person from its harm. Muslims say it after mistakes, three times after each prayer, and as daily remembrance, following the Prophet's example.

### How do you write astaghfirullah in Arabic?

Astaghfirullah is written أستغفر الله in Arabic. A longer form is أستغفر الله وأتوب إليه, "I seek God's forgiveness and turn to Him in repentance," which the Prophet used in Sahih al-Bukhari 6307. The standard English spelling is astaghfirullah; "astaghfirallah" is a common misspelling.

### What is Sayyidul Istighfar?

Sayyid al-Istighfar, "the master of seeking forgiveness," is a dua taught by the Prophet in Sahih al-Bukhari 6306. It begins "Allahumma anta Rabbi, la ilaha illa anta." The Prophet said that whoever says it with firm faith during the day and dies before evening, or at night and dies before morning, will be among the people of Paradise.

### What are the benefits of istighfar?

The Quran links istighfar to God's forgiveness (4:110), rain, wealth, children, gardens and rivers (71:10-12), good provision (11:3) and added strength (11:52). Sahih al-Bukhari 6306 promises Paradise to one who says Sayyid al-Istighfar with certainty. A popular hadith about relief from every worry (Abu Dawud 1518) is graded weak.

### How many times should you say astaghfirullah a day?

There is no fixed obligatory number. The Prophet said astaghfirullah three times after each prayer (Sahih Muslim 591), and he sought forgiveness more than 70 times a day (Sahih al-Bukhari 6307) or 100 times a day (Sahih Muslim 2702). Many Muslims aim for at least 100 daily, following his example.

### Is it wrong to say astaghfirullah when shocked?

Saying astaghfirullah on hearing something wrong or offensive is common and has a sound basis, since the speaker distances themselves from wrongdoing and turns to God. Teachers caution against using it to judge others, as a joke, or without meaning. Ideally it remains a sincere request for one's own forgiveness.

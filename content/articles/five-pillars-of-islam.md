The five pillars of Islam are the five core acts of worship every Muslim is asked to observe: the declaration of faith (shahada), prayer (salah), obligatory charity (zakat), fasting in Ramadan (sawm) and the pilgrimage to Makkah (hajj). They are called "pillars" because the Prophet Muhammad described Islam as a building that stands upon these five supports.

For Muslims, the pillars are the framework of daily, yearly and lifelong worship. For anyone exploring Islam, they are the quickest way to see what being a Muslim looks like in practice.

## Key takeaways

- The five pillars of Islam, in order, are shahada, salah, zakat, sawm and hajj.
- The list comes from the hadith "Islam is built upon five," recorded in both Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim.
- The phrase "five pillars" is not in the Quran, but every one of the five acts is commanded in the Quran.
- Salah is prayed five times a day, zakat is paid once a year, sawm is observed for one month a year and hajj is required once in a lifetime for those able.
- The pillars rest on belief; Muslims see them as expressions of faith, not a replacement for it.

## What are the five pillars of Islam? The hadith behind them

The five pillars are listed in a hadith narrated by Abdullah ibn Umar and recorded in both Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim. The Prophet (peace be upon him) said that Islam is built upon five: testifying that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, establishing prayer, giving zakat, performing hajj to the House and fasting in Ramadan. In one narration in Sahih Muslim, fasting is mentioned before hajj, which is the order most people learn today.

The same five appear in the hadith of Jibril, recorded in Sahih Muslim from Umar ibn al-Khattab. The angel Jibril (Gabriel) came in the form of a man and asked, "Tell me about Islam." The Prophet answered with these five acts, then went on to explain faith (the six articles of faith) and excellence (*ihsan*), which he described as worshipping God as though you see Him.

"Allah" is the Arabic word for God, used by Arabic-speaking Muslims and Christians alike.

## The five pillars of Islam in order

Here are the five pillars of Islam in their traditional order, with their meaning.

1. **Shahada (declaration of faith)** – bearing witness that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is His messenger.
2. **Salah (prayer)** – performing the five daily prayers at their set times.
3. **Zakat (purifying charity)** – giving a fixed share of savings to those in need each year.
4. **Sawm (fasting)** – fasting from dawn to sunset throughout the month of Ramadan.
5. **Hajj (pilgrimage)** – travelling to Makkah to perform the pilgrimage once in a lifetime, if physically and financially able.

### 1. Shahada, the declaration of faith

The shahada is the statement "There is no god but Allah, and Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah." Saying it sincerely, with understanding, is how a person enters Islam, and Muslims repeat it many times a day in prayer. It has two parts: the oneness of God and the prophethood of Muhammad.

Both halves come straight from the Quran:

> Allah witnesses that there is no deity except Him, and [so do] the angels and those of knowledge - [that He is] maintaining [creation] in justice. There is no deity except Him, the Exalted in Might, the Wise.
>
> — [Quran 3:18](/surah/ali-imran/18)

The second half, "Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah," is the opening phrase of [Quran 48:29](/surah/al-fath/29). Every other pillar builds on this one, because worship only has meaning when it is directed to the one God.

### 2. Salah, the five daily prayers

Salah is the ritual prayer Muslims perform five times a day: Fajr (dawn), Dhuhr (midday), Asr (afternoon), Maghrib (sunset) and Isha (night). Each prayer includes standing, recitation of the Quran, bowing and prostration, and is done facing the Kaaba in Makkah.

The Quran commands prayer dozens of times and ties it to fixed times:

> And when you have completed the prayer, remember Allah standing, sitting, or [lying] on your sides. But when you become secure, re-establish [regular] prayer. Indeed, prayer has been decreed upon the believers a decree of specified times.
>
> — [Quran 4:103](/surah/an-nisa/103)

Verses such as [Quran 17:78](/surah/al-isra/78) and [Quran 11:114](/surah/hud/114) point to the prayer times. The exact number of five daily prayers and how to perform them come from the Prophet's teaching and practice, which scholars have explained in detail. The Quran also describes the purpose of prayer: "Indeed, prayer prohibits immorality and wrongdoing" ([Quran 29:45](/surah/al-ankabut/45)).

### 3. Zakat, the obligatory charity

Zakat means "purification" and "growth." It is a yearly payment, commonly calculated as 2.5 percent of savings held for a full lunar year above a minimum threshold called the *nisab*. It is not a voluntary donation but a duty, and voluntary giving (*sadaqah*) is encouraged on top of it.

The Quran pairs zakat with prayer again and again, as in "And establish prayer and give zakah and bow with those who bow" ([Quran 2:43](/surah/al-baqarah/43)). It also names who may receive it:

> Zakah expenditures are only for the poor and for the needy and for those employed to collect [zakah] and for bringing hearts together [for Islam] and for freeing captives [or slaves] and for those in debt and for the cause of Allah and for the [stranded] traveler - an obligation [imposed] by Allah. And Allah is Knowing and Wise.
>
> — [Quran 9:60](/surah/at-tawbah/60)

The detailed rules on which wealth is counted and how much is due are explained by scholars from the Prophet's teaching.

### 4. Sawm, fasting in Ramadan

During Ramadan, the ninth month of the Islamic lunar calendar, adult Muslims who are able go without food, drink and marital relations from dawn until sunset. The fast is also meant to train patience, self-control and gratitude, and many Muslims read the whole Quran during the month.

> O you who have believed, decreed upon you is fasting as it was decreed upon those before you that you may become righteous -
>
> — [Quran 2:183](/surah/al-baqarah/183)

The Quran explains why Ramadan was chosen and builds in ease for the sick and travellers:

> The month of Ramadhan [is that] in which was revealed the Qur'an, a guidance for the people and clear proofs of guidance and criterion. So whoever sights [the new moon of] the month, let him fast it; and whoever is ill or on a journey - then an equal number of other days. Allah intends for you ease and does not intend for you hardship and [wants] for you to complete the period and to glorify Allah for that [to] which He has guided you; and perhaps you will be grateful.
>
> — [Quran 2:185](/surah/al-baqarah/185)

The holiest night of the month is the Night of Decree; read more in our guide to [Laylatul Qadr](/learn/laylatul-qadr).

### 5. Hajj, the pilgrimage to Makkah

Hajj is the pilgrimage to the Kaaba in Makkah, performed during the month of Dhul Hijjah. It is required once in a lifetime for every adult Muslim who is physically and financially able. Pilgrims wear simple white garments, which removes signs of wealth and status, and follow rites that trace the footsteps of Abraham, his wife Hagar and their son Ishmael.

> In it are clear signs [such as] the standing place of Abraham. And whoever enters it shall be safe. And [due] to Allah from the people is a pilgrimage to the House - for whoever is able to find thereto a way. But whoever disbelieves - then indeed, Allah is free from need of the worlds.
>
> — [Quran 3:97](/surah/ali-imran/97)

Abraham himself was told to call people to this pilgrimage: "And proclaim to the people the Hajj [pilgrimage]" ([Quran 22:27](/surah/al-hajj/27)).

## Are the five pillars of Islam in the Quran?

The phrase "five pillars" does not appear in the Quran; it comes from the hadith "Islam is built upon five." However, each pillar is clearly commanded in the Quran, as the verses above show. The Quran gives the command and the core purpose, and the Prophet's teaching (the Sunnah) shows how to carry it out, such as how many units each prayer has or how hajj is performed step by step.

The pillars also sit alongside belief. They are the visible side of the six articles of faith described in our guide [What is Islam](/learn/what-is-islam).

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the five pillars of Islam in order?

The five pillars of Islam in order are shahada (declaration of faith), salah (prayer), zakat (charity), sawm (fasting in Ramadan) and hajj (pilgrimage to Makkah). This order follows the well-known hadith "Islam is built upon five" in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim. Some narrations mention hajj before fasting, but the meaning is the same.

### Are the five pillars of Islam mentioned in the Quran?

The exact phrase "five pillars" is not found in the Quran; it comes from the Prophet Muhammad's hadith. Each individual pillar, however, is commanded in the Quran, including the testimony of God's oneness (3:18), prayer (4:103), zakat (9:60), fasting (2:183) and hajj (3:97).

### What does the word "pillar" mean in the five pillars of Islam?

The Prophet compared Islam to a structure "built upon five," so the five acts are its supports, or pillars. Without them, a Muslim's practice is incomplete. The Arabic word used by scholars is *arkan*, meaning pillars or foundations.

### Do all Muslims have to perform hajj?

Hajj is required only once in a lifetime and only for adult Muslims who are physically and financially able to travel. The Quran describes it as due "for whoever is able to find thereto a way" (3:97). Those who are unable are not obliged to go.

### How much is zakat?

Zakat is commonly calculated as 2.5 percent of qualifying savings that have been held for a full lunar year above the minimum threshold called the nisab. Different rates apply to other kinds of wealth such as crops and livestock, and scholars explain these details.

### What is the difference between the five pillars and the six articles of faith?

The five pillars are actions Muslims perform, while the six articles of faith are beliefs Muslims hold. The articles of faith are belief in God, angels, revealed books, messengers, the Last Day and divine decree. Both are listed together in the hadith of Jibril in Sahih Muslim.

To convert to Islam, a person sincerely believes in one God and in Muhammad as His final messenger, then says the shahada: "I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, and I bear witness that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah." There is no fee, no exam, no required ceremony, and no need for a priest or official. Saying it sincerely makes you Muslim.

Becoming Muslim is a big personal step, and it is normal to have questions about witnesses, family, names and what comes next. This guide answers those questions plainly. It is written for people who have already been thinking about Islam, and it does not ask you to rush. The Quran itself says that faith must be freely chosen ([Quran 2:256](/surah/al-baqarah/256)).

## Key takeaways

- The only requirement to enter Islam is sincere belief expressed in the shahada, the testimony of faith that the Prophet named first among the five pillars (Sahih al-Bukhari 8).
- Witnesses are recommended for community and paperwork reasons, but scholars of the main schools agree they are not needed for the shahada to be valid before God.
- A hadith in Sahih Muslim 121 teaches that accepting Islam wipes out the sins that came before it.
- The Prophet told a new convert, Qays ibn Asim, to take a ritual bath (ghusl), recorded in Sunan Abi Dawud 355; scholars differ on whether it is obligatory or recommended.
- Changing your name is not required; the Prophet changed only names with a bad meaning (Sahih Muslim 2139).

## What does converting to Islam actually require?

Converting requires two things: belief in the heart and testimony with the tongue. The belief is that there is one God, Allah, with no partner or equal, and that Muhammad (peace be upon him) is His messenger. That is the core of the faith; the wider beliefs, such as angels, revealed books and the Day of Judgment, are explained in our guide to [what Muslims believe](/learn/what-do-muslims-believe).

The testimony is the shahada. Here it is in transliteration and translation:

**Transliteration:** Ash-hadu an la ilaha illa-llah, wa ash-hadu anna Muhammadan rasulu-llah.

**English:** I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, and I bear witness that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah.

You may say it in Arabic, in English, or in both. Many people say the Arabic first, slowly, then repeat it in their own language so they know they meant every word. Our guide to the [meaning of the shahada](/learn/shahada-meaning) explains each word and its source in the Quran.

What Islam does *not* require is just as important:

- No payment or registration fee.
- No baptism-style ritual or ceremony.
- No clergy. Islam has scholars and imams, but no one stands between a person and God.
- No test of knowledge. You do not need to know Arabic or how to pray yet.
- No change of ethnicity, culture or nationality.

## How to take your shahada, step by step

1. **Make sure you believe it.** Read about Islam, ask questions and take your time. A shahada said to please someone else, or under pressure, is not what Islam asks for.
2. **Form the intention.** Decide in your heart that you are entering Islam for God's sake.
3. **Say the shahada.** Say it clearly and sincerely, either alone or in front of others.
4. **Take a ritual bath (ghusl).** Wash your whole body with the intention of purification. This can be done straight after, or soon after.
5. **Begin learning to pray.** Start with the five daily prayers, learning the basics step by step.
6. **Connect with other Muslims.** A local mosque or a new Muslim support group can help with the next steps.

### Can you take your shahada at home?

Yes. The shahada is valid wherever it is said, including at home, alone, or over a video call with a friend or imam. Many people take their shahada privately first because they want to be sure before telling anyone. Some later repeat it at a mosque to be welcomed by the community, which is perfectly fine.

### Do you need witnesses to take shahada?

No. Scholars across the Sunni schools agree that witnesses are not a condition for the shahada to be valid before God. Witnesses are still useful. Many mosques issue a conversion certificate, and a certificate may be requested later, for example when applying to perform Hajj or Umrah, or for a Muslim marriage. If you take your shahada at home, you can visit a mosque later to get one.

### Can you be Muslim without taking your shahada?

Belief in the heart matters most, and scholars discuss the case of someone who believes but has not yet said the words. The mainstream position is that the testimony with the tongue is required when a person is able to say it, because it is how faith is declared and how others know to treat you as Muslim. If you already believe, there is no reason to wait: say it, even privately.

## Is converting to Islam different for women?

No. The steps are the same for women and men. A woman does not need permission from a father, husband or guardian to become Muslim, and she says the same shahada. The Quran addresses believing men and believing women side by side throughout, as in [Quran 47:19](/surah/muhammad/19).

Women often ask about clothing. Most scholars teach that a Muslim woman covers her hair and body in public, but converts are rarely expected to change everything overnight, and many adopt the headscarf gradually as their understanding grows. Our guide on [why Muslim women wear the hijab](/learn/why-do-muslim-women-wear-hijab) explains the reasoning and the different views. Questions about an existing marriage to a non-Muslim spouse depend on individual circumstances, so it is best to ask a qualified, trusted scholar.

## What happens to your past sins when you convert?

Islam teaches that conversion is a fresh start. When the companion Amr ibn al-As asked, as he accepted Islam, for his past sins to be forgiven, the Prophet asked him whether he knew that Islam wipes out what came before it (Sahih Muslim 121). The Quran makes a similar promise:

> Except for those who repent, believe and do righteous work. For them Allah will replace their evil deeds with good. And ever is Allah Forgiving and Merciful.
>
> — [Quran 25:70](/surah/al-furqan/70)

Many new Muslims find this one of the most moving parts of conversion. God's name [At-Tawwab](/names/at-tawwab), "the One who accepts repentance," is a good place to start reading about His mercy.

## What to do after saying the shahada: your first 30 days

There is no single official checklist, but this is a realistic order that many mosques and teachers recommend. The aim is steady progress, not perfection.

| When | What to focus on | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Day 1 | Take a ritual bath (ghusl) | The Prophet told a new convert to wash (Sunan Abi Dawud 355) |
| Week 1 | Learn wudu and the basic movements of prayer | Prayer is the first practice after the shahada |
| Weeks 1 to 2 | Memorize Surah al-Fatihah | It is recited in every unit of prayer |
| Weeks 2 to 3 | Pray the five daily prayers, even if simply | Consistency matters more than length |
| Weeks 3 to 4 | Find a mosque, teacher or new Muslim group | Community makes the change sustainable |
| Ongoing | Read the Quran in translation, a little each day | It is the foundation of belief and practice |

### Ghusl: the bath of a new Muslim

Ghusl means washing the whole body with the intention of purification. Scholars differ over its status for a new Muslim: the Hanbali and Maliki schools treat it as obligatory, while the Shafi'i and Hanafi schools generally treat it as recommended unless the person was in a state that already requires ghusl. In practice, most new Muslims simply do it. Either way, your shahada is valid before the bath.

### Learning to pray

Prayer (*salah*) is the most important practice after the shahada. Start with how to purify yourself before prayer in our guide on [how to perform wudu](/learn/how-to-perform-wudu), then follow [how to pray salah step by step](/learn/how-to-pray-salah). If you are wondering why the day has five prayers, see [why Muslims pray five times a day](/learn/why-muslims-pray-five-times-a-day).

It is fine to pray simply at first. In a hadith graded hasan by al-Albani, a man told the Prophet he could not memorize anything of the Quran, and the Prophet taught him short phrases of praise to say in its place (Sunan Abi Dawud 832). God "does not charge a soul except [with that within] its capacity" ([Quran 2:286](/surah/al-baqarah/286)).

### Surah al-Fatihah

[Surah al-Fatihah](/surah/al-fatihah) is the opening chapter of the Quran, only seven short verses, and is recited in every unit of every prayer. Our guide on [the meaning of Surah al-Fatihah](/learn/surah-al-fatihah-meaning) walks through it line by line, and [how to read the Quran](/learn/how-to-read-the-quran) has tips for beginners who cannot yet read Arabic.

## Do you have to change your name when you become Muslim?

No. Many converts choose an Arabic name, but it is a personal choice, not a religious requirement. The Prophet changed names only when the meaning was bad; for example, he renamed a woman called Asiyah ("disobedient") as Jamilah ("beautiful"), recorded in Sahih Muslim 2139. If your name has a good or neutral meaning, you can keep it.

Your family name stays the same. The Quran tells believers to call people "by [the names of] their fathers" ([Quran 33:5](/surah/al-ahzab/5)), so converts keep their family connection in their name.

## Telling your family, and common fears

For many people, the hardest part of converting is not belief but family. Some converts tell relatives straight away; others wait until they feel settled. There is no religious obligation to announce it at a particular time, and choosing a calm moment is wise.

Islam does not ask converts to cut off non-Muslim parents. The Quran tells believers that even if their parents push them away from their faith, they should still "accompany them in [this] world with appropriate kindness" ([Quran 31:15](/surah/luqman/15)). Being visibly kinder, more patient and more honest after conversion is often what reassures a worried family most.

Common fears, briefly:

- **"I don't know enough."** No one does at the start. Learning is part of being Muslim for life.
- **"I will make mistakes in prayer."** Everyone does. Keep learning and keep praying.
- **"I'll lose my identity."** You keep your name, culture, most of your food (pork and alcohol are the main exceptions), and your family.
- **"I'll be alone."** Support groups exist precisely for this; see below.

## Where can new Muslims find support?

Feeling isolated is one of the most common struggles for converts, so reaching out early helps. These organisations focus on supporting new Muslims:

- [New Muslim Academy](https://newmuslimacademy.org): free online classes, live sessions with instructors and one-to-one mentorship for new Muslims anywhere.
- [WhyIslam](https://www.whyislam.org): an outreach project of the Islamic Circle of North America (ICNA) that answers questions and connects people with local volunteers and mosques in the United States.
- [New Muslims Project](https://www.islamic-foundation.org.uk/projects/new-muslims-project): founded in 1993 by the Islamic Foundation in the UK, offering courses, a newsletter and advice.
- [Convert Muslim Foundation](https://www.convertmuslimfoundation.org.uk): a UK charity led by converts, which runs the Convert Care network of local support groups.

A local mosque is also a good first stop. Many have a new Muslim class or a volunteer who looks after recent converts.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do you take your shahada?

You take your shahada by sincerely saying: "I bear witness that there is no god but Allah, and I bear witness that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah." In Arabic: *Ash-hadu an la ilaha illa-llah, wa ash-hadu anna Muhammadan rasulu-llah*. You can say it in Arabic, English or both, alone or with others. Belief in the heart is what makes it count.

### Do you need witnesses to take shahada?

No. Scholars agree that witnesses are not required for the shahada to be valid before God. They are recommended because they help you join the community and get a conversion certificate, which may be requested later for Hajj, Umrah or a Muslim marriage. You can take your shahada privately and get a certificate from a mosque afterward.

### Can I take my shahada at home?

Yes. The shahada is valid wherever it is said sincerely, including at home and alone. Many people take it privately first and later repeat it at a mosque to be welcomed by the community and receive a certificate. There is no required place, ceremony or official who must be present.

### How do you convert to Islam as a woman?

A woman converts to Islam in exactly the same way as a man: by sincerely believing and saying the shahada. She does not need permission from a father, husband or guardian. Many women adopt Islamic dress gradually as they learn. Questions about an existing marriage should be taken to a qualified scholar, since the answer depends on circumstances.

### What should you do after saying the shahada?

After saying the shahada, take a ritual bath (ghusl), then begin learning wudu, the five daily prayers and Surah al-Fatihah. Connect with a mosque or new Muslim support group, and read the Quran in a translation you understand. Go at a steady pace; Islam does not expect a new Muslim to learn everything at once.

### Do you have to change your name if you convert to Islam?

No. Changing your name is optional. The Prophet only changed names with a bad meaning, as recorded in Sahih Muslim 2139. If your first name has a good or neutral meaning, you can keep it, and you always keep your family name. Some converts choose an Arabic name because they like its meaning.

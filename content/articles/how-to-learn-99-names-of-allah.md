The 99 Names of Allah, called *al-Asma' al-Husna* ("the Most Beautiful Names"), are titles that describe God's attributes, such as Ar-Rahman (the Beneficent) and Al-Alim (the All-Knowing). The easiest way to learn them is in small themed groups of about ten names a week, pairing each name with its meaning, a Quran verse and a daily dua.

The Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) promised Paradise to whoever "counts" these names, so Muslims of every age set out to memorize them. Below you will find what that promise means, a ten-week plan, memory techniques that work for adults and children, and the full list of all 99 names in order with their meanings.

## Key takeaways

- A hadith in Sahih al-Bukhari (2736) and Sahih Muslim (2677) reports that Allah has ninety-nine names, and whoever "enumerates" them will enter Paradise.
- Another narration of the same hadith in Sahih al-Bukhari (7392) uses the wording "whoever memorized them," which is why memorizing the list is a common devotional goal.
- Scholars such as Ibn al-Qayyim explained that "enumerating" the names means more than reciting them: it includes understanding their meanings and calling on God by them.
- The Quran commands believers to "invoke Him by them" ([Quran 7:180](/surah/al-araf/180)), so the names are meant to be used in dua, not only learned.
- The familiar list of 99 names and its order come from a narration in Jami' at-Tirmidhi (3507), which hadith scholars graded weak, so the order is a helpful convention rather than a revealed sequence.

## What does "whoever enumerates them enters Paradise" mean?

The hadith, narrated by Abu Hurayrah, says that Allah has "ninety-nine names, one hundred minus one," and whoever *ahsaha* them will enter Paradise. It appears in Sahih al-Bukhari 2736 and 7392 and in Sahih Muslim 2677. Another version, Sahih al-Bukhari 6410, adds that "Allah is witr (One) and loves the witr."

**Ihsa'** (*ihsa'*, إحصاء) literally means counting or enumerating something completely. English translations of Bukhari render it in different ways: "whoever knows them," "whoever memorized them," and "whoever believes in their meanings and acts accordingly." Each translation captures part of what scholars understood.

The classical scholar Ibn al-Qayyim described three levels of *ihsa'*:

1. **Learning the names themselves**, being able to recite and count them.
2. **Understanding their meanings**, what each name tells us about God.
3. **Calling on God by them**, in praise and in requests, as the Quran commands.

So the goal is not a memory test. A person who can recite all 99 but never thinks about them has done the first step only. A person who knows a few dozen well, and turns to God through them, is closer to the spirit of the hadith. Ideally, you aim for all three.

## How to learn the 99 Names of Allah: a 10-week plan

Learning 99 unfamiliar Arabic words at once is hard. Learning ten related words a week is easy. The plan below groups the names by theme, so each week's names explain each other and stick together in memory.

| 10 | Protection, truth and timing | Al-Hafiz, Al-Muhaymin, Al-Waliyy, Al-Wakil, Al-Haqq, Al-Wajid, Al-Muqaddim, Al-Mu'akhkhir |

A simple routine for each week:

1. **Day 1:** Read the ten names aloud with their meanings. Open each one's page on Quran Universe and read the verse where it appears.
2. **Days 2 to 4:** Learn three or four names a day. Recite the new ones after each prayer, then add them to the ones before.
3. **Days 5 and 6:** Recite the whole group from memory, then test yourself on meanings.
4. **Day 7:** Review every name learned so far, from week one onward.

The weekly review is what makes the plan work. Without it, early names fade by week five. If ten names a week feels like too many, take two weeks per group. The order in which you learn them does not matter for the reward.

## Memory techniques that work

### Pair each name with its verse

Most of the names appear in the Quran, often at the end of a verse that shows them in action. Al-Ghafur and Ar-Raheem close [Quran 39:53](/surah/az-zumar/53), the verse that tells sinners not to despair of God's mercy. Al-Awwal, Al-Akhir, Az-Zahir and Al-Batin all appear in one line, [Quran 57:3](/surah/al-hadid/3). Linking a name to a story or verse gives it a place to live in your memory.

Every name page on Quran Universe, such as [Al-Wadud](/names/al-wadud) or [Ar-Razzaq](/names/ar-razzaq), lists the verses where the name or its root appears.

### Use the names in your daily dua

The Quran says, "to Allah belong the best names, so invoke Him by them" ([Quran 7:180](/surah/al-araf/180)). Match the name to the need. When asking forgiveness, call on Him as [Al-Ghaffar](/names/al-ghaffar) or [At-Tawwab](/names/at-tawwab). When asking for provision, call Him [Ar-Razzaq](/names/ar-razzaq) or [Al-Wahhab](/names/al-wahhab). When worried, call on [Al-Wakil](/names/al-wakil), the Trustee. Names you use daily become names you never forget.

### Learn paired names together

Several names come as pairs of opposites: Al-Qabid and Al-Basit (the Constrictor and the Reliever), Al-Mu'izz and Al-Muzill (the One who honors and the One who humbles), Ad-Darr and An-Nafi'. Many scholars advise mentioning these together, since the pair expresses God's full control over both sides of a matter. Pairs are also easier to memorize than single names.

### Flashcards and a self-quiz

Write the Arabic name on one side and the meaning on the other, or use a spaced-repetition app. Shuffle the deck and test yourself both ways: name to meaning, and meaning to name. A quick 99 Names of Allah quiz at the end of each week, done alone or with family, shows exactly which names need more review.

### Listen and recite

Many people first learned the names through a recited or sung version. Listening while you commute, then reciting along, uses sound and rhythm as a memory aid. Make sure the recording uses correct pronunciation.

### Tips for teaching children

Children learn best with fewer names and more stories. Start with names they can feel, such as Ar-Rahman, Al-Khaliq, Ar-Razzaq and As-Sami', and ask them where they see each one in their day. Rewarding each completed group keeps them motivated.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do the 99 Names of Allah have to be learned in order?

No. The familiar order comes from a narration in Jami' at-Tirmidhi that hadith scholars graded weak, and the authentic hadith in Bukhari and Muslim gives no order. Learning the names in themed groups is perfectly valid. Many people still memorize the traditional order because it is widely recited and makes group recitation easier.

### Is the list of 99 names fixed?

Not exactly. The authentic hadith confirms the number 99 but does not list the names. The common list comes from Tirmidhi's narration, and scholars such as Ibn Hajar and Ibn Uthaymin compiled alternative lists from the Quran and authentic Sunnah. Most names overlap across lists, but a few differ from one list to another.

### Does Allah have more than 99 names?

Yes, according to most scholars. A dua taught by the Prophet, recorded in Musnad Ahmad, asks God by every name He has, including names He "kept in the knowledge of the unseen." Scholars explain that the hadith about 99 names describes a special set with a special reward, not the total number of His names.

### How long does it take to memorize the 99 Names of Allah?

At ten names a week, about ten weeks, with steady daily review. Some people learn them faster by reciting a set each day after prayer, and children often learn them through a recited version. Understanding the meanings takes longer, and many Muslims keep deepening that understanding for life.

### What is the best way to memorize the 99 Names of Allah?

Learn them in small themed groups, recite new names after each daily prayer, and review everything once a week. Link each name to a verse where it appears and use it in your own dua. Test yourself with flashcards in both directions: Arabic to meaning, and meaning to Arabic.

### Is there a reward for learning the 99 Names of Allah?

Yes. Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim record the Prophet's promise that whoever enumerates the ninety-nine names will enter Paradise. Scholars explain that this includes memorizing the names, understanding their meanings and calling on God by them. Knowing God through His names also deepens faith, love and trust in Him.

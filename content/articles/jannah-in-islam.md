Jannah is the Arabic word for Paradise in Islam. It literally means "garden," and it refers to the eternal home God has prepared for those who believe and do good. The Quran describes it as gardens with flowing rivers, peace without fear or sorrow, and, greater than every pleasure, the approval of God and the joy of seeing Him.

Jannah is one of the most frequently mentioned subjects in the Quran, and it shapes how Muslims think about hope, effort and loss. This guide explains what the word means, how the Quran and authentic hadith describe Paradise, what is known about its levels and gates, and answers common questions such as whether families are reunited and whether animals go to Jannah.

## Key takeaways

- *Jannah* means "garden" in Arabic, and the Quran repeatedly describes Paradise as "gardens beneath which rivers flow" ([Quran 2:25](/surah/al-baqarah/25)).
- Sahih al-Bukhari 2790 states that Paradise has one hundred levels and that al-Firdaus is the best and highest of them.
- Sahih al-Bukhari 3257 states that Paradise has eight gates, one of which, ar-Rayyan, is reserved for those who fasted.
- God says in a hadith qudsi that He has prepared for His servants "what no eye has seen, no ear has heard, and no human heart has imagined" (Sahih al-Bukhari 3244; Sahih Muslim 2824).
- The Quran calls God's approval greater than all the gardens of Paradise ([Quran 9:72](/surah/at-tawbah/72)), and Sahih Muslim 181 says seeing God will be dearer to its people than anything they were given.

## What does Jannah mean?

**Jannah** (*jannah*, جنة) comes from an Arabic root, j-n-n, that carries the idea of something hidden or covered. A garden is called *jannah* because its trees are so dense that they cover the ground beneath them. The same root gives the word *jinn*, beings hidden from human sight.

In Islamic belief, Jannah is a real place, already created, that believers will enter after the Day of Judgment. It is not only a spiritual state or a metaphor. At the same time, Muslims hold that its true nature is beyond anything people can picture now, so the Quran's descriptions point toward a reality rather than fully capturing it.

## What does the Quran say Jannah is like?

The Quran describes Paradise in vivid, physical terms, often in the same breath as a call to faith and good deeds. Its rivers are one of the best-known images:

> Is the description of Paradise, which the righteous are promised, wherein are rivers of water unaltered, rivers of milk the taste of which never changes, rivers of wine delicious to those who drink, and rivers of purified honey, in which they will have from all [kinds of] fruits and forgiveness from their Lord, like [that of] those who abide eternally in the Fire and are given to drink scalding water that will sever their intestines?
>
> — [Quran 47:15](/surah/muhammad/15)

Other verses describe shade and mild weather, where people "will not see therein any [burning] sun or [freezing] cold" ([Quran 76:13](/surah/al-insan/13)), with fruit hanging within easy reach, couches and gold vessels, and garments of silk. Its size is described as "as wide as the heavens and earth" ([Quran 3:133](/surah/ali-imran/133)). The wine of Paradise causes no headache or intoxication ([Quran 56:19](/surah/al-waqiah/19)), unlike worldly wine.

Two surahs give especially rich portraits. [Surah ar-Rahman](/surah/ar-rahman) describes two pairs of gardens with flowing springs and fruit of every kind, and our guide to [Surah ar-Rahman](/learn/surah-ar-rahman) walks through its famous refrain. [Surah al-Waqiah](/surah/al-waqiah) describes the "foremost" near to God, reclining on lined couches, and is explained in our article on [Surah al-Waqiah](/learn/surah-al-waqiah).

### What is in Jannah beyond physical pleasure?

The Quran stresses inner peace as much as outward comfort. God says He will remove all resentment from the hearts of the people of Paradise, so they sit "as brothers, on thrones facing each other" ([Quran 15:47](/surah/al-hijr/47)). There is no idle talk, no fatigue and no fear. Sahih Muslim 2835 adds that its people eat and drink without any of the bodily discomforts of this world, and that praising God comes to them as naturally as breathing.

The greatest reward is spiritual. After describing the gardens, the Quran says "approval from Allah is greater" ([Quran 9:72](/surah/at-tawbah/72)). Sahih al-Bukhari 6549 reports that God will tell the people of Paradise He is pleased with them and will never be displeased with them again.

### What does Jannah look like? "What no eye has seen"

When people ask what Jannah looks like, Muslim scholars usually answer with a hadith qudsi, a saying of the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) in which he reports God's words. Recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari 3244 and Sahih Muslim 2824, God says He has prepared for His righteous servants what no eye has seen, no ear has heard, and no human heart has ever imagined. The Prophet then recited this verse:

> And no soul knows what has been hidden for them of comfort for eyes as reward for what they used to do.
>
> — [Quran 32:17](/surah/as-sajdah/17)

The Quran's images are real, in other words, but they are a starting point. The reality is described using words people know, for something they have never seen.

## What are the levels of Jannah?

Jannah is not a single level for everyone. People are raised in rank according to their faith and deeds. Sahih al-Bukhari 2790 records the Prophet saying that Paradise has one hundred levels, with the distance between each two like the distance between heaven and earth.

### What is Jannah al-Firdaus?

In the same hadith, the Prophet said: when you ask God for Paradise, ask for **al-Firdaus** (*al-firdaws*, الفردوس), because it is the best and highest part of Paradise. A narrator adds that he believed the Prophet also said that above it is the Throne of the Most Merciful, and from it the rivers of Paradise flow. This is why many Muslims end their prayers with "O Allah, I ask You for al-Firdaus."

Even the lowest level is beyond imagination. Sahih al-Bukhari 6571 describes the very last person to leave the Fire and enter Paradise. God tells him he will have the equal of the whole world and ten times more, and the man, overwhelmed, asks whether God is joking with him.

## What are the 8 gates of Jannah?

Sahih al-Bukhari 3257 states that Paradise has eight gates and that one of them is called **ar-Rayyan**, through which only those who fasted will enter. Sahih al-Bukhari 1897 names four gates, each linked to a deed a person was devoted to:

| Gate | Who is called through it | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gate of Prayer | Those devoted to prayer | Sahih al-Bukhari 1897 |
| Gate of Jihad | Those who strove in God's cause | Sahih al-Bukhari 1897 |
| Ar-Rayyan | Those devoted to fasting | Sahih al-Bukhari 1896, 1897 |
| Gate of Charity | Those devoted to giving | Sahih al-Bukhari 1897 |

In the same hadith, Abu Bakr asked whether anyone would be called from all the gates. The Prophet replied, "Yes, and I hope you will be one of them." Sahih Muslim 234 adds that whoever performs ablution well and then testifies that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is His servant and messenger will have all eight gates opened, to enter by whichever they wish.

Authentic hadith name only these four gates. Lists of the remaining four that circulate online (such as a gate for those who restrain their anger) rely on weaker reports or on scholars' inferences, so they should be treated with caution.

## Common questions about Jannah

### Will families be together in Jannah?

The Quran promises that believers will be joined by their believing descendants, even if those descendants did less, so that families are reunited at the higher level:

> And those who believed and whose descendants followed them in faith - We will join with them their descendants, and We will not deprive them of anything of their deeds. Every person, for what he earned, is retained.
>
> — [Quran 52:21](/surah/at-tur/21)

### Will people get bored in Jannah?

The Quran addresses this directly. The people of Paradise "will not desire from it any transfer" ([Quran 18:108](/surah/al-kahf/108)), and they will have "whatever the souls desire and [what] delights the eyes" ([Quran 43:71](/surah/az-zukhruf/71)). Boredom comes from limits and repetition, and scholars explain that Paradise has neither.

### Do animals go to Jannah?

No verse or authentic hadith says that pets or animals in general enter Paradise. Sahih Muslim 2582 teaches that animals will be gathered on the Day of Judgment and that even a hornless sheep will be given justice against a horned one, which shows God's complete fairness. Many scholars, citing later reports, hold that animals then return to dust. Others say the matter is unknown. What the Quran does promise is that people in Paradise will have whatever they desire.

## Jannah and Jahannam

The Quran almost always mentions Paradise alongside its opposite, **Jahannam**, or Hell. Jahannam is described as a fire "whose fuel is people and stones" ([Quran 66:6](/surah/at-tahrim/6)), and Sahih al-Bukhari 3265 says worldly fire is only one part in seventy of its heat. The pairing is deliberate: hope and fear together are meant to keep a believer balanced, neither careless nor despairing.

| Aspect | Jannah | Jahannam |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meaning | Garden | Hell, the Fire |
| Who enters | Those who believe and do good, by God's mercy | Those who reject God and persist in wrongdoing |
| Described with | Rivers, shade, peace, nearness to God | Fire, boiling water, regret |
| Key verses | Quran 47:15, 55:46, 56:10-26 | Quran 66:6, 74:26-30 |

Islam also teaches that no one enters Paradise through deeds alone, but through God's mercy. The names [Ar-Rahman](/names/ar-rahman) and [Al-Ghafur](/names/al-ghafur), the Most Merciful and the Great Forgiver, appear throughout these passages. Our guide to [whether God forgives all sins](/learn/does-god-forgive-all-sins) explains repentance in more detail.

## What to say when someone dies: "May Allah grant them Jannah"

When a Muslim dies, friends and family commonly say "May Allah grant him Jannah" or "May Allah grant her Jannah al-Firdaus." This is a prayer, not a statement of certainty. Islamic belief holds that only God knows anyone's final outcome, so Muslims pray for the deceased rather than declare where they are.

Alongside it, people say *Inna lillahi wa inna ilayhi raji'un* and "may Allah have mercy on them" (*rahimahullah*). ## Frequently asked questions

### What is the meaning of Jannah?

Jannah means "garden" in Arabic and is the Islamic word for Paradise. It refers to the eternal home God has prepared for believers after the Day of Judgment. The Quran describes it as gardens beneath which rivers flow, free of fear, sorrow and pain, where people will have whatever they desire and the approval of God.

### What are the levels of Jannah?

According to Sahih al-Bukhari 2790, Paradise has one hundred levels, and the distance between each two levels is like the distance between heaven and earth. People are placed in levels according to their faith and deeds. The highest and best level is al-Firdaus, which the Prophet told believers to ask God for.

### What is Jannah al-Firdaus?

Al-Firdaus is the highest and best level of Paradise. In Sahih al-Bukhari 2790, the Prophet Muhammad told Muslims to ask God for al-Firdaus specifically, and a narrator reported that above it is the Throne of the Most Merciful and from it the rivers of Paradise flow. Muslims often pray to be granted al-Firdaus.

### What are the 8 gates of Jannah?

Sahih al-Bukhari 3257 confirms Paradise has eight gates. Sahih al-Bukhari 1897 names four: the gate of prayer, the gate of jihad, ar-Rayyan for those who fasted, and the gate of charity. Lists naming the other four come from weaker reports or scholarly inference. Sahih Muslim 234 says all eight open for one who does wudu well and testifies to faith.

### Do animals go to Jannah?

No verse or authentic hadith states that animals enter Paradise. Sahih Muslim 2582 says animals will be gathered on the Day of Judgment so that wrongs between them are settled. Many scholars hold that they then become dust, while others say only God knows. The Quran promises that people in Paradise will have whatever they desire.

### What is the difference between Jannah and Jahannam?

Jannah is Paradise, the eternal garden of reward and peace for believers. Jahannam is Hell, the Fire of punishment for those who reject God and persist in wrongdoing. The Quran usually mentions them together so that believers hold hope and fear in balance, and it teaches that entry into Jannah is ultimately by God's mercy.

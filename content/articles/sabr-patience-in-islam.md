Sabr is the Arabic word for patience, but its meaning is wider than waiting. In Islam, sabr is holding yourself steady for God's sake: staying firm in doing what is right, holding back from what is wrong, and bearing hardship without despair or complaint against God. The Quran says, "Indeed, Allah is with the patient" (2:153).

Sabr is one of the most frequently praised qualities in the Quran, and one of the most misunderstood. It does not mean passivity, suppressing every emotion, or accepting injustice. This guide explains what sabr means, its three types, the key Quran verses on patience, and how the prophets lived it.

## Key takeaways

- The Quran tells believers to "seek help through patience and prayer. Indeed, Allah is with the patient" ([Quran 2:153](/surah/al-baqarah/153)).
- The patient "will be given their reward without account," meaning without measure ([Quran 39:10](/surah/az-zumar/10)).
- The Prophet Muhammad said that "patience is at the first stroke of a calamity," according to Sahih al-Bukhari 1283.
- Sahih al-Bukhari 1469 records the Prophet saying that no one is given a blessing better and greater than patience.
- The Prophet Yaqub (Jacob) responded to losing his son with *sabrun jamil*, "patience is most fitting" ([Quran 12:18](/surah/yusuf/18)), while still voicing his grief to God.

## What does sabr mean?

**Sabr** (*sabr*, صبر) comes from an Arabic root meaning to restrain, hold back or confine. Applied to the soul, it means holding yourself back from panic, from sin, and from giving up. Classical scholars described it as restraining the self from despair, the tongue from complaint, and the limbs from acts of frustration such as tearing clothes or striking oneself in grief.

Sabr is active. It is the strength to keep doing the right thing when it is hard, which is why the Quran pairs it with words like "persevere" and "endure":

> O you who have believed, persevere and endure and remain stationed and fear Allah that you may be successful.
>
> — [Quran 3:200](/surah/ali-imran/200)

One of God's names is [as-Sabur, the Patient One](/names/as-sabur), who does not hurry to punish and gives people time to return. Human patience is a small reflection of that attribute.

## What are the three types of sabr?

Classical scholars, including Ibn al-Qayyim in his book on patience, *'Uddat al-Sabirin*, divided sabr into three types. This framework is widely taught because it shows that patience is needed in every part of life, not only during disasters.

| Type of sabr | What it means | Everyday example | Related verse |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Patience in obeying God | Staying consistent in worship and duty when it is tiring | Getting up for the dawn prayer on a cold morning | [Quran 20:132](/surah/taha/132) |
| Patience in avoiding sin | Holding back from what is forbidden when it is tempting | Walking away from gossip, cheating or an unlawful relationship | [Quran 79:40](/surah/an-naziat/40) |
| Patience with God's decree | Bearing loss, illness or hardship without despair | Grieving a loss while trusting God's wisdom | [Quran 2:155](/surah/al-baqarah/155) |

The third type rests on belief in divine decree (*qadar*), one of the six articles of faith explained in [what Muslims believe](/learn/what-do-muslims-believe). Many people only think of this third type. But the first two are daily tests, and scholars often considered them harder, because no one around you notices when you choose them.

## Quran verses about patience

The Quran returns to patience again and again. These are the verses most often quoted.

### Seek help through patience and prayer

> O you who have believed, seek help through patience and prayer. Indeed, Allah is with the patient.
>
> — [Quran 2:153](/surah/al-baqarah/153)

This verse treats patience as a resource you draw on, alongside prayer. It also promises God's company: He is "with the patient."

### The test and the response

> And We will surely test you with something of fear and hunger and a loss of wealth and lives and fruits, but give good tidings to the patient, Who, when disaster strikes them, say, "Indeed we belong to Allah, and indeed to Him we will return." Those are the ones upon whom are blessings from their Lord and mercy. And it is those who are the [rightly] guided.
>
> — [Quran 2:155–157](/surah/al-baqarah/155)

The phrase in verse 156, *Inna lillahi wa inna ilayhi raji'un* (إِنَّا لِلَّهِ وَإِنَّآ إِلَيۡهِ رَٰجِعُونَ), is what Muslims say on hearing of a death or any loss. It reframes loss: what we had was always God's, and we too are returning to Him. Sahih Muslim (918) adds a prayer to say with it: "O Allah, reward me for my affliction and give me something better than it in exchange."

### Reward without measure

> Say, "O My servants who have believed, fear your Lord. For those who do good in this world is good, and the earth of Allah is spacious. Indeed, the patient will be given their reward without account."
>
> — [Quran 39:10](/surah/az-zumar/10)

Most good deeds have a stated reward. Patience is promised a reward "without account," beyond counting.

### Patience is part of success

Surah al-Asr, one of the shortest chapters, says all of humanity is in loss except those who believe, do good, and "advised each other to truth and advised each other to patience" ([Quran 103:3](/surah/al-asr/3)). Patience here is something a community encourages in one another, not only a private virtue.

## How did the prophets show patience?

The Quran teaches sabr largely through stories. Two prophets stand out. You can read about all of them in our guide to the [prophets in the Quran](/learn/prophets-in-the-quran).

### Prophet Yaqub (Jacob) and "beautiful patience"

When Yaqub's sons returned with Yusuf's shirt stained with false blood, he answered: "so patience is most fitting. And Allah is the one sought for help against that which you describe" ([Quran 12:18](/surah/yusuf/18)). The Arabic is *fasabrun jamil*, often translated "beautiful patience." Years later, losing a second son, he said the same words again ([Quran 12:83](/surah/yusuf/83)).

Yaqub's patience did not mean hiding grief. The Quran says "his eyes became white from grief" ([Quran 12:84](/surah/yusuf/84)), and he said, "I only complain of my suffering and my grief to Allah" ([Quran 12:86](/surah/yusuf/86)). Scholars draw a clear lesson: crying and expressing sorrow to God are compatible with sabr. What sabr rules out is complaining *about* God to people, or losing hope in Him.

### Prophet Ayyub (Job) and patience in illness

Ayyub was tested with severe illness and the loss of his family. His prayer is a model of how to ask for relief without complaint:

> And [mention] Job, when he called to his Lord, "Indeed, adversity has touched me, and you are the Most Merciful of the merciful."
>
> — [Quran 21:83](/surah/al-anbya/83)

God responded and restored what he had lost ([Quran 21:84](/surah/al-anbya/84)), and described him in another passage: "Indeed, We found him patient, an excellent servant" ([Quran 38:44](/surah/sad/44)). His example shows that asking God for relief is not impatience.

## What did the Prophet Muhammad teach about patience?

Several authentic hadith shape how Muslims understand sabr.

- **Patience is at the first strike.** The Prophet passed a woman weeping at a grave and told her to fear God and be patient. Not recognizing him, she told him to go away. When she later came to apologize, he said, "Verily, the patience is at the first stroke of a calamity" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1283). Real patience is tested in the first moment, before time softens the blow.
- **Patience can be learned.** "Whoever remains patient, Allah will make him patient. Nobody can be given a blessing better and greater than patience" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1469).
- **Hardship expiates sins.** "No fatigue, nor disease, nor sorrow, nor sadness, nor hurt, nor distress befalls a Muslim, even if it were the prick he receives from a thorn, but that Allah expiates some of his sins for that" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5641).
- **Good in every situation.** "Strange are the ways of a believer for there is good in every affair of his." If something pleasing happens, he gives thanks, and that is good for him; if hardship comes, he is patient, and that is good for him too (Sahih Muslim 2999).

The last hadith pairs patience with gratitude. Muslims see them as two halves of faith: gratitude in ease, patience in hardship.

## Sabr vs giving up: what patience is not

Because sabr is often translated simply as "patience," people sometimes confuse it with resignation. The Quran and Sunnah draw the lines clearly.

| Sabr is | Sabr is not |
| --- | --- |
| Staying steady while doing everything you can | Doing nothing and calling it trust |
| Grieving and crying while trusting God | Pretending not to feel pain |
| Telling God about your pain | Complaining about God to people |
| Seeking medical care, therapy or help | Refusing treatment as a sign of faith |
| Standing up to injustice with wisdom | Accepting abuse or oppression silently |
| Hoping for relief | Despairing of God's mercy |

The Prophet himself wept when his infant son Ibrahim was dying, saying, "The eyes are shedding tears and the heart is grieved, and we will not say except what pleases our Lord" (Sahih al-Bukhari 1303). Sabr works together with effort and trust, which is explored in our guide to [tawakkul, trust in God](/learn/tawakkul-trust-in-god).

## How to build patience

1. **Say the words at the first moment.** Train yourself to say *Inna lillahi wa inna ilayhi raji'un* the instant something goes wrong.
2. **Turn to prayer.** The Quran pairs patience with prayer in 2:153. A short prayer or prostration in a hard moment is the Quran's own prescription.
3. **Remember the promise.** "With hardship [will be] ease," the Quran repeats twice ([Quran 94:5–6](/surah/ash-sharh/5)).
4. **Practice the small types.** Patience in obedience and in avoiding sin builds the strength you need for larger trials.
5. **Ask for it.** Pray as the believers with Talut did: "Our Lord, pour upon us patience" ([Quran 2:250](/surah/al-baqarah/250)).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the meaning of sabr?

Sabr is Arabic for patience, from a root meaning to restrain or hold back. In Islam it means holding yourself steady for God's sake: persevering in doing good, refraining from sin, and bearing hardship without despair or complaint against God. It is an active strength, not passive waiting.

### What are the three types of sabr?

Scholars such as Ibn al-Qayyim describe three types: patience in obeying God (staying consistent in worship), patience in avoiding sin (resisting temptation), and patience with God's decree (bearing loss and hardship). The first two are daily tests, while the third comes with trials such as illness, grief or poverty.

### What does the Quran say about patience?

The Quran says "Allah is with the patient" (2:153), tells believers to seek help "through patience and prayer," and promises that the patient "will be given their reward without account" (39:10). It gives good news to those who respond to loss by saying "Indeed we belong to Allah, and indeed to Him we will return" (2:156).

### What is sabrun jamil?

Sabrun jamil means "beautiful patience." It comes from the words of Prophet Yaqub (Jacob) when his sons claimed a wolf had eaten his son Yusuf: "so patience is most fitting" (Quran 12:18). Scholars explain it as patience without complaining about God to people, even while grieving and turning to God with one's sorrow.

### Is crying against sabr in Islam?

No. Crying and feeling sorrow are natural and do not contradict sabr. The Prophet Muhammad wept at the death of his son Ibrahim (Sahih al-Bukhari 1303), and Prophet Yaqub grieved so deeply that his eyes turned white (Quran 12:84). What sabr excludes is despair, complaint against God, and acts of uncontrolled anger or self-harm in grief.

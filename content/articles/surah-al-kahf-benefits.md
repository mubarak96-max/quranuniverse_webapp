The two best-known benefits of Surah Kahf are light that shines for the reader from one Friday to the next, and protection from the Dajjal (the False Messiah) for whoever memorizes its first ten verses. Both are reported in authentic hadith, which is why millions of Muslims read Surah al-Kahf every Friday.

Beyond these rewards, Surah al-Kahf is a gripping chapter built around four stories. Each one shows a different kind of test that people face, and how faith helps them pass it.

## Key takeaways

- Surah al-Kahf is the 18th surah of the Quran, with 110 verses, and was revealed in Mecca.
- Reading Surah al-Kahf on Friday brings light between the two Fridays, according to a hadith recorded by al-Hakim and al-Bayhaqi and graded authentic by al-Albani.
- Sahih Muslim reports that memorizing the first ten verses of Surah al-Kahf protects a person from the Dajjal, and one narration mentions the last ten verses.
- The four stories of Surah al-Kahf are the youths of the cave, the owner of two gardens, Musa (Moses) and al-Khidr, and Dhul-Qarnayn.
- Many scholars read the four stories as four trials: of faith, wealth, knowledge and power.

## Surah Kahf meaning and background

*Al-Kahf* means "The Cave", named after the cave where a group of believing youths took refuge. [Surah al-Kahf](/surah/al-kahf) is a Meccan surah, revealed before the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) migrated to Madinah, at a time when the small Muslim community faced pressure and ridicule. You can learn more about this period in our guide to [Meccan and Medinan surahs](/learn/meccan-and-medinan-surahs).

The surah opens by praising God for the Quran itself.

> [All] praise is [due] to Allah, who has sent down upon His Servant the Book and has not made therein any deviance.
>
> — [Quran 18:1](/surah/al-kahf/1)

According to an account recorded by the early historian Ibn Ishaq and cited by Ibn Kathir, the leaders of Quraysh sent questions to test the Prophet. They asked about young men who disappeared long ago and a man who travelled to the east and the west. The Prophet said he would answer the next day but did not add "if God wills", and revelation paused for some days. When the surah came, it included this gentle teaching.

> And never say of anything, "Indeed, I will do that tomorrow," Except [when adding], "If Allah wills." And remember your Lord when you forget [it] and say, "Perhaps my Lord will guide me to what is nearer than this to right conduct."
>
> — [Quran 18:23–24](/surah/al-kahf/23)

This is the origin of the Muslim habit of saying *in sha' Allah* ("if God wills") about future plans.

## Benefits of Surah Kahf on Friday

The companion Abu Sa'id al-Khudri reported that the Prophet said whoever reads Surat al-Kahf on Friday, light will shine for him between the two Fridays. This hadith is recorded by al-Hakim and al-Bayhaqi, and scholars such as al-Albani graded it authentic.

Scholars describe this light as guidance and blessing during the week, and as light on the Day of Judgment. The weekly habit also keeps the surah's lessons fresh.

### When to read Surah Kahf on Friday

In the Islamic calendar, each day begins at sunset. Many scholars therefore explain that the Friday window runs from sunset on Thursday until sunset on Friday. Within that time, the surah can be read in one sitting or spread across the day, whichever is easier.

## First 10 verses of Surah Kahf and the Dajjal

In Islamic belief, the Dajjal is a great deceiver who will appear before the end of time, claiming divine power and leading many astray. The Prophet described his trial as the greatest test humanity will face.

Sahih Muslim records from Abu al-Darda that the Prophet said whoever memorizes ten verses from the beginning of Surat al-Kahf will be protected from the Dajjal. Some narrators of the same hadith said "from the end of Surat al-Kahf", so many Muslims learn both. In another long hadith in Sahih Muslim, the Prophet advised that whoever meets the Dajjal should recite the opening verses of al-Kahf over him.

Why these verses? Scholars note that the opening passage reminds us that the world's attractions are a test, which is exactly how the Dajjal will deceive people.

> Indeed, We have made that which is on the earth adornment for it that We may test them [as to] which of them is best in deed.
>
> — [Quran 18:7](/surah/al-kahf/7)

The first ten verses also begin the story of the youths who held on to their faith against a powerful, hostile society. Their prayer in verse 10 is one many Muslims memorize.

> [Mention] when the youths retreated to the cave and said, "Our Lord, grant us from Yourself mercy and prepare for us from our affair right guidance."
>
> — [Quran 18:10](/surah/al-kahf/10)

## The four stories of Surah al-Kahf and their lessons

### 1. The people of the cave: the trial of faith

A group of young men lived among people who worshipped idols and persecuted believers in one God. They fled to a cave, and God caused them to sleep for 309 years (verse 25) before waking them as a sign that He can raise the dead.

> It is We who relate to you, [O Muhammad], their story in truth. Indeed, they were youths who believed in their Lord, and We increased them in guidance.
>
> — [Quran 18:13](/surah/al-kahf/13)

Readers from a Christian background may recognize a similar story, the Seven Sleepers of Ephesus. The Quran tells the story with its own focus and notes that only God knows their exact number (verse 22). The lesson is to protect your faith, keep good company, and trust God when the world turns against you.

### 2. The owner of two gardens: the trial of wealth

Verses 32 to 44 describe a man blessed with two rich gardens who grew arrogant. He told his poorer friend he was greater in wealth and doubted the Hour would ever come. His friend reminded him to say *ma sha' Allah*, "what Allah willed", recognizing that every blessing comes from God. The gardens were destroyed, and the man was left full of regret.

> Wealth and children are [but] adornment of the worldly life. But the enduring good deeds are better to your Lord for reward and better for [one's] hope.
>
> — [Quran 18:46](/surah/al-kahf/46)

The lesson is gratitude and humility. Wealth is a test that can disappear overnight, while good deeds last.

### 3. Musa and al-Khidr: the trial of knowledge

Sahih al-Bukhari explains the background. When the Prophet Musa was asked who was the most knowledgeable person, he said "I", and God told him there was a servant at the junction of the two seas who knew more. The Quran describes this man without naming him; the hadith calls him al-Khidr.

> And they found a servant from among Our servants to whom we had given mercy from us and had taught him from Us a [certain] knowledge.
>
> — [Quran 18:65](/surah/al-kahf/65)

Al-Khidr damaged a boat, took the life of a boy, and repaired a wall for a town that had refused them food. Musa protested each time. Al-Khidr then revealed the hidden wisdom: the boat was saved from a king who seized good ships, the boy's believing parents would be given a better child, and the wall protected a treasure belonging to two orphans.

> And as for the wall, it belonged to two orphan boys in the city, and there was beneath it a treasure for them, and their father had been righteous. So your Lord intended that they reach maturity and extract their treasure, as a mercy from your Lord. And I did it not of my own accord. That is the interpretation of that about which you could not have patience."
>
> — [Quran 18:82](/surah/al-kahf/82)

Scholars agree that al-Khidr acted on direct command from God, as he says, and that no one else may take such actions. The lesson is humility about our own knowledge and patience with events whose wisdom we cannot yet see. Musa's story is one of many covered in our article on [prophets in the Quran](/learn/prophets-in-the-quran).

### 4. Dhul-Qarnayn: the trial of power

Dhul-Qarnayn, "the one with two horns", was a righteous ruler whom God gave great power. He travelled to the far west and far east, treating people with justice. Then he helped a community threatened by Gog and Magog (*Ya'juj* and *Ma'juj*) by building a mighty barrier of iron and copper, refusing their payment. Scholars have long debated his historical identity, and the Quran does not name him further.

> [Dhul-Qarnayn] said, "This is a mercy from my Lord; but when the promise of my Lord comes, He will make it level, and ever is the promise of my Lord true."
>
> — [Quran 18:98](/surah/al-kahf/98)

The lesson is that power is a trust. A believer uses it to serve and protect others, and credits success to God rather than to himself.

## What connects the four stories?

Each story shows a test and the way through it. The youths survived the test of faith through sincere prayer and good companions. The gardens show wealth tested by gratitude, Musa's journey shows knowledge tested by humility, and Dhul-Qarnayn shows power tested by justice.

Many scholars point out that these are the very temptations the Dajjal will use: false religion, wealth, apparent knowledge and power. That may be why this surah is tied to protection from him. The surah's final verse sums up the way through every trial.

> Say, "I am only a man like you, to whom has been revealed that your god is one God. So whoever would hope for the meeting with his Lord - let him do righteous work and not associate in the worship of his Lord anyone."
>
> — [Quran 18:110](/surah/al-kahf/110)

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the benefits of reading Surah Kahf on Friday?

According to a hadith recorded by al-Hakim and al-Bayhaqi, and graded authentic by al-Albani, whoever reads Surah al-Kahf on Friday will have light shining for them between the two Fridays. Scholars understand this light as guidance and blessing during the week and light on the Day of Judgment.

### When should Surah Kahf be read on Friday?

Because the Islamic day begins at sunset, many scholars explain that the time for reading Surah al-Kahf runs from sunset on Thursday until sunset on Friday. It can be read all at once or in parts during that window.

### Do the first 10 verses of Surah Kahf protect from the Dajjal?

Yes. Sahih Muslim reports that the Prophet Muhammad said whoever memorizes ten verses from the beginning of Surah al-Kahf will be protected from the Dajjal. Some narrations of the same hadith say the last ten verses, so many Muslims memorize both.

### What are the four stories of Surah al-Kahf?

The four stories are the people of the cave, the owner of two gardens, the Prophet Musa and al-Khidr, and the just ruler Dhul-Qarnayn. Many scholars see them as four trials, of faith, wealth, knowledge and power, each paired with the attitude that overcomes it.

### What does Surah al-Kahf mean?

*Al-Kahf* is Arabic for "The Cave". The surah takes its name from the cave where a group of young believers sheltered from persecution and slept for 309 years. It is the 18th surah of the Quran and has 110 verses.

Surah Mulk (Surah al-Mulk, "The Dominion") is the 67th surah of the Quran, a chapter of 30 verses that the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) said will intercede for its reader until they are forgiven. Muslims recite it every night before sleep, following the Prophet's own habit, and his companions called it a protector from the punishment of the grave.

It is short enough to memorize, yet it covers some of the Quran's biggest questions: why we live and die, who holds power over the universe, and what happens when we meet God.

## Key takeaways

- Surah al-Mulk is the 67th surah of the Quran, with 30 verses, revealed in Mecca, and it opens the 29th juz.
- A hadith in Jami at-Tirmidhi and Sunan Abi Dawud, graded hasan (good), says a surah of thirty verses interceded for a man until he was forgiven, and names it as Surah al-Mulk.
- The companion Ibn Mas'ud described Surah al-Mulk as "the protector" from the punishment of the grave, in a report graded hasan by al-Albani.
- Jami at-Tirmidhi reports that the Prophet would not sleep until he had recited Surah as-Sajdah and Surah al-Mulk.
- Its central message is that life and death were created as a test to see who is best in deeds.

## What is Surah al-Mulk?

*Al-Mulk* means "the dominion" or "the kingdom", taken from the first verse. The surah is also known as *Tabarak*, its first word, and the 29th juz of the Quran is often called "Juz Tabarak" because it begins with this surah. The phrase "Surah e Mulk", common in South Asia, simply means "the Surah of al-Mulk" in Urdu and Persian style.

[Surah al-Mulk](/surah/al-mulk) was revealed in Mecca, where the Prophet's early message focused on belief in one God and the afterlife. Our guide to [Meccan and Medinan surahs](/learn/meccan-and-medinan-surahs) explains how the two periods differ. It opens with two verses that frame everything that follows.

> Blessed is He in whose hand is dominion, and He is over all things competent - [He] who created death and life to test you [as to] which of you is best in deed - and He is the Exalted in Might, the Forgiving -
>
> — [Quran 67:1–2](/surah/al-mulk/1)

"Allah", used throughout the surah, is the Arabic word for God, and Arab Christians use it too.

## Surah Mulk meaning in English: a summary

The 30 verses flow through a few clear themes. Reading the surah in English with these in mind helps the message land.

### Life as a test (verses 1–2)

The surah begins with God's complete control over all things, then states why life exists at all. Notice that death is mentioned before life. Scholars explain that keeping death in mind is what helps a person live well, and that the test is about the *best* deeds, not simply the most.

### A flawless creation (verses 3–5)

The reader is invited to look at the sky, again and again, and search for a flaw.

> [And] who created seven heavens in layers. You do not see in the creation of the Most Merciful any inconsistency. So return [your] vision [to the sky]; do you see any breaks? Then return [your] vision twice again. [Your] vision will return to you humbled while it is fatigued.
>
> — [Quran 67:3–4](/surah/al-mulk/3)

This is a challenge to reflect, not just believe. The order in the universe is presented as evidence of a wise Creator.

### Regret of those who refused to think (verses 6–11)

The surah describes people in the Fire admitting their mistake. Their regret is striking: it is not that they lacked evidence, but that they did not listen or reflect.

> And they will say, "If only we had been listening or reasoning, we would not be among the companions of the Blaze."
>
> — [Quran 67:10](/surah/al-mulk/10)

### God knows what is hidden (verses 12–14)

Those who fear God even when no one sees them are promised "forgiveness and great reward." Then comes a short but profound question.

> And conceal your speech or publicize it; indeed, He is Knowing of that within the breasts. Does He who created not know, while He is the Subtle, the Acquainted?
>
> — [Quran 67:13–14](/surah/al-mulk/13)

### The earth, the birds and daily provision (verses 15–22)

The surah points to ordinary things we take for granted: ground that is stable enough to walk on, food to eat, and birds held in the air.

> Do they not see the birds above them with wings outspread and [sometimes] folded in? None holds them [aloft] except the Most Merciful. Indeed He is, of all things, Seeing.
>
> — [Quran 67:19](/surah/al-mulk/19)

### Closing questions (verses 23–30)

The final verses ask the listener to consider their total dependence on God. The surah ends with a question about something as basic as water.

> Say, "Have you considered: if your water was to become sunken [into the earth], then who could bring you flowing water?"
>
> — [Quran 67:30](/surah/al-mulk/30)

## Surah Mulk benefits

Surah al-Mulk is one of the few surahs with authentic reports about specific benefits.

### It intercedes for its reader

Abu Hurayrah reported that the Prophet said a surah of the Quran, thirty verses long, interceded for a man until he was forgiven, and it is "Blessed is He in whose hand is dominion." This hadith is recorded in Jami at-Tirmidhi, who graded it hasan (good), as well as in Sunan Abi Dawud and Sunan Ibn Majah.

### Surah Mulk protection from grave punishment

In Islamic belief, the soul passes through a stage between death and resurrection called *barzakh*, and the grave can be a place of comfort or of punishment. The companion Abdullah ibn Mas'ud said that Surah al-Mulk is "the protector" (*al-mani'ah*) that guards against the punishment of the grave. This report is recorded by an-Nasa'i and al-Hakim, and al-Albani graded it hasan.

Jami at-Tirmidhi also records a story of a companion who pitched a tent over a grave without knowing it and heard someone inside reciting Surah al-Mulk. Scholars consider that narration's chain weaker, so the report from Ibn Mas'ud is the stronger basis for this benefit.

These rewards are understood to come to those who recite the surah regularly, believe in it and act on it, not to those who treat it as a charm.

## Surah Mulk benefits before sleeping

Jabir ibn Abdullah reported that the Prophet would not go to sleep until he had recited *Alif Lam Mim Tanzil*, which is [Surah as-Sajdah](/surah/as-sajdah), and *Tabarak alladhi biyadihil mulk*, which is Surah al-Mulk. This is recorded in Jami at-Tirmidhi and graded authentic by al-Albani.

This is why so many Muslims make Surah al-Mulk the last thing they recite at night. At a steady pace it takes only a few minutes, and the surah's theme of death and accountability fits the moment of sleep, which Muslims see as a small reminder of death.

Many people combine it with other bedtime recitations taught by the Prophet, such as [Ayatul Kursi](/learn/ayatul-kursi) and the three protective surahs explained in our guide to the [four Quls](/learn/four-quls).

## Lessons from Surah al-Mulk in daily life

- **Live with purpose.** Life is a test of the quality of your deeds, so aim for excellence rather than quantity.
- **Reflect on the world.** The surah repeatedly asks you to look up, look around and think.
- **Be the same in private and in public.** God knows what is hidden in the heart.
- **Practise gratitude.** Stable ground, food, water and the flight of birds are all signs of God's care.
- **Do not wait for regret.** The people of the Fire wished they had listened and reasoned while they still could.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the benefits of Surah Mulk?

According to a hadith in Jami at-Tirmidhi and Sunan Abi Dawud, Surah al-Mulk interceded for a man until he was forgiven. The companion Ibn Mas'ud also described it as a protector from the punishment of the grave. The Prophet recited it every night before sleeping, making it part of the Sunnah.

### Does Surah Mulk protect from the punishment of the grave?

The companion Abdullah ibn Mas'ud said that Surah al-Mulk is "the protector" from the punishment of the grave, in a report recorded by an-Nasa'i and al-Hakim and graded hasan by al-Albani. Scholars explain that this benefit is for those who recite it regularly with faith and act on its message.

### When is the best time to recite Surah Mulk?

The best-known time is at night before sleeping. Jami at-Tirmidhi reports that the Prophet would not sleep until he had recited Surah as-Sajdah and Surah al-Mulk. It can, of course, be recited at any time of day.

### What does Surah e Mulk mean?

"Surah e Mulk" is a South Asian way of saying "the Surah of al-Mulk". *Al-Mulk* means "the dominion" or "the kingdom", referring to God's complete authority over everything. The surah is also called *Tabarak*, after its first word.

### How many verses are in Surah al-Mulk?

Surah al-Mulk has 30 verses. It is the 67th surah of the Quran, was revealed in Mecca, and is the first surah of the 29th juz, which is often called Juz Tabarak.

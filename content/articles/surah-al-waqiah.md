The main benefits of Surah Waqiah are the reward of reciting the Quran and the powerful way it reminds the reader of death, the Day of Judgment and God's role as the true Provider. The popular narration that reading it every night protects a person from poverty is graded weak (da'if) by many hadith scholars, so it should not be treated as a guaranteed promise from the Prophet.

Surah al-Waqi'ah (also searched as "Surah Waqa" or "Surah Waqia") is the 56th chapter of the Quran. This guide explains its meaning, its most important passages, and what the hadith literature actually says about its virtues.

## Key takeaways

- Surah al-Waqi'ah is the 56th surah of the Quran, has 96 verses and was revealed in Mecca.
- Its name means "The Event" or "The Inevitable," referring to the Day of Judgment.
- It describes humanity divided into three groups: the foremost, the companions of the right, and the companions of the left (Quran 56:7–10).
- A narration from Ibn Mas'ud that reciting it nightly prevents poverty is graded weak by many scholars, including al-Albani.
- An authentic narration in Jami at-Tirmidhi reports that the Prophet said this surah, among others, had turned his hair grey.

## What does Surah al-Waqiah mean?

*Al-Waqi'ah* comes from an Arabic root meaning "to fall" or "to happen." It refers to the event that will certainly happen: the end of the world and the resurrection. The surah opens by stating that there is no doubting it.

> When the Occurrence occurs, There is, at its occurrence, no denial. It will bring down [some] and raise up [others].
>
> — [Quran 56:1–3](/surah/al-waqiah/1)

The third verse is one of the surah's key ideas. The Day of Judgment "will bring down" some people and "raise up" others. Commentators explain that many who were powerful in this world will be humbled, and many who were overlooked will be honored, because what counts is faith and deeds.

The surah comes directly after [Surah Ar-Rahman](/learn/surah-ar-rahman), and the two share themes. Both describe Paradise in vivid detail and both speak of two kinds of reward for the righteous.

## The three groups on the Day of Judgment

After the earth shakes and the mountains crumble to dust, the surah says that people will be sorted into three kinds.

> And you become [of] three kinds: Then the companions of the right - what are the companions of the right? And the companions of the left - what are the companions of the left? And the forerunners, the forerunners - Those are the ones brought near [to Allah]
>
> — [Quran 56:7–11](/surah/al-waqiah/7)

"Allah" is the Arabic word for God, used by Arab Christians as well as Muslims. The three groups are:

1. **The forerunners (as-sabiqun).** Those who raced ahead in faith and good deeds. They are "brought near" to God and receive the highest rank. The surah describes them as "A [large] company of the former peoples And a few of the later peoples" ([Quran 56:13–14](/surah/al-waqiah/13)).
2. **The companions of the right (ashab al-yamin).** The righteous believers, who receive their record of deeds in their right hand. They enjoy shade, water, fruit and peace ([Quran 56:27–40](/surah/al-waqiah/27)).
3. **The companions of the left (ashab ash-shimal).** Those who rejected the truth, described as having lived "indulging in affluence" and denying the resurrection ([Quran 56:41–47](/surah/al-waqiah/41)).

For the reader, the passage works like a mirror. It invites each person to ask which group they are moving toward, while there is still time to change.

## God as Creator and Provider

The middle of the surah answers people who doubted that God could bring the dead back to life. It asks a series of simple questions about everyday things: human birth, farming, water and fire.

> And have you seen that [seed] which you sow? Is it you who makes it grow, or are We the grower?
>
> — [Quran 56:63–64](/surah/al-waqiah/63)

> And have you seen the water that you drink? Is it you who brought it down from the clouds, or is it We who bring it down? If We willed, We could make it bitter, so why are you not grateful?
>
> — [Quran 56:68–70](/surah/al-waqiah/68)

The argument is gentle but strong. People plant seeds and light fires, but they do not create the life in a seed or the water in the clouds. The One who gives all of this provision every day can certainly create again. The section ends with a call to worship: "So exalt the name of your Lord, the Most Great" ([Quran 56:74](/surah/al-waqiah/74)).

## What does Surah al-Waqiah say about the Quran?

The surah then swears an oath "by the setting of the stars" and describes the Quran itself.

> Indeed, it is a noble Qur'an In a Register well-protected; None touch it except the purified. [It is] a revelation from the Lord of the worlds.
>
> — [Quran 56:77–80](/surah/al-waqiah/77)

Ibn Kathir records that many early commentators, including reports from Ibn Abbas, understood "the purified" here as the angels, who handle the original record in heaven. Many scholars also connect the verse, alongside hadith, with the etiquette of being in a state of ritual purity when touching a copy of the Quran. The detailed rules are explained in books of Islamic law.

## The moment of death

The closing passage turns from the end of the world to the end of each individual life. It describes the soul reaching the throat at death, with family gathered around, unable to hold it back.

> Then why, when the soul at death reaches the throat And you are at that time looking on - And Our angels are nearer to him than you, but you do not see -
>
> — [Quran 56:83–85](/surah/al-waqiah/83)

The surah then returns to its three groups, this time at the moment of death. Those brought near receive "rest and bounty and a garden of pleasure," the companions of the right are greeted with "Peace for you," and the deniers face the Fire ([Quran 56:88–94](/surah/al-waqiah/88)). It ends exactly as the middle section did, with the call to glorify "your Lord, the Most Great."

## Surah Waqiah benefits: what does the hadith say?

Because this surah is so widely recited, many claims about it circulate online. Here is what the major sources say.

### The narration about poverty (weak)

The most famous claim comes from a report attributed to Ibn Mas'ud: "Whoever recites Surah al-Waqi'ah every night will never be afflicted by poverty." It is found in collections such as al-Bayhaqi's *Shu'ab al-Iman*. Hadith scholars, from early critics to al-Albani in modern times, have graded its chain as weak (da'if) because of problems with its narrators.

Some scholars allowed acting on weak narrations about virtues under certain conditions, and so many Muslims continue to read Surah al-Waqi'ah each night. That practice is a good habit in itself. However, the promise of protection from poverty should not be presented as an authentic saying of the Prophet.

### The narration about grey hair (authentic)

A report in Jami at-Tirmidhi says that Abu Bakr told the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him), "You have become grey." The Prophet replied that Surah Hud, al-Waqi'ah, al-Mursalat, an-Naba and at-Takwir had turned his hair grey. At-Tirmidhi graded it hasan gharib, and al-Albani graded it authentic. Scholars explain that these surahs weighed on him because of their intense descriptions of the Day of Judgment.

### The reward of recitation

Like every chapter of the Quran, reciting Surah al-Waqi'ah earns reward. A hadith in Jami at-Tirmidhi, graded authentic, says that each letter recited brings a good deed, multiplied by ten.

## Surah Waqiah benefits for rizq

People often search for Surah Waqiah benefits for rizq (provision or sustenance). The surah does have a real and deep connection to provision, but it comes from its message rather than from a weak narration.

The surah reminds the reader that God makes the crops grow and sends down the rain. It also contains a warning about people who receive God's provision and respond with denial: "And make [the thanks for] your provision that you deny [the Provider]?" ([Quran 56:82](/surah/al-waqiah/82)). Its lesson is that gratitude is the right response to provision.

Elsewhere, the Quran names clear causes of increased provision. These include mindfulness of God, about whom it says God "will provide for him from where he does not expect" ([Quran 65:3](/surah/at-talaq/3)), seeking forgiveness ([Quran 71:10–12](/surah/nuh/10)), and gratitude: "If you are grateful, I will surely increase you [in favor]" ([Quran 14:7](/surah/ibrahim/7)). Reading Surah al-Waqi'ah with understanding can strengthen all three.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the benefits of Surah Waqiah?

Reciting Surah al-Waqi'ah earns the reward of reciting the Quran, and its message strengthens belief in the Hereafter and gratitude to God as Provider. The popular claim that reading it nightly prevents poverty comes from a narration graded weak by many hadith scholars.

### Is the Surah Waqiah poverty hadith authentic?

Most hadith scholars, including al-Albani, have graded the narration "Whoever recites Surah al-Waqi'ah every night will never be afflicted by poverty" as weak (da'if). Many Muslims still recite the surah nightly as a good practice, but the promise itself is not established as an authentic saying of the Prophet.

### What are the three groups in Surah al-Waqiah?

Quran 56:7–11 describes people on the Day of Judgment as three kinds: the forerunners, who are brought nearest to God; the companions of the right, who are the righteous believers; and the companions of the left, who rejected the truth.

### How many verses are in Surah al-Waqiah?

Surah al-Waqi'ah has 96 verses. It is the 56th chapter of the Quran and was revealed in Mecca.

### When should Surah Waqiah be recited?

There is no authentic hadith fixing a specific time for reciting Surah al-Waqi'ah. Many Muslims choose to read it at night, following the weak narration about poverty, and this is a common habit, but it can be recited at any time.

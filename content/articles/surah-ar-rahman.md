Surah Rahman (Surah Ar-Rahman, also spelled Surah Rehman) is the 55th chapter of the Quran, and its name means "The Most Merciful." It has 78 verses that list God's blessings in creation, in this life and in Paradise, and it repeats one question 31 times: "So which of the favors of your Lord would you deny?"

Many people know this surah from its beautiful recitation, which is among the most listened-to in the world. This guide explains what it actually says, what its famous refrain means, and which of its popular "benefits" come from authentic sources.

## Key takeaways

- Surah Ar-Rahman is the 55th surah of the Quran and has 78 verses.
- Its refrain, "Fabi-ayyi ala'i rabbikuma tukadhdhiban," means "So which of the favors of your Lord would you deny?" and appears 31 times.
- The refrain addresses two audiences at once, humans and jinn, which is why the Arabic uses a dual form.
- The surah moves from blessings in nature, to the end of the world and judgment, to two pairs of gardens in Paradise.
- The popular saying that Ar-Rahman is "the bride of the Quran" comes from a narration that scholars grade as weak.

## What is the meaning of Surah Ar-Rahman?

The surah opens with one of the names of God, *ar-Rahman*, "the Most Merciful" or "the Entirely Merciful." In Arabic, the word comes from the same root as *rahm*, a mother's womb, suggesting a mercy that is vast and nurturing. "Allah" is the Arabic word for God, used by Arab Christians as well as Muslims.

> The Most Merciful Taught the Qur'an, Created man, [And] taught him eloquence.
>
> — [Quran 55:1–4](/surah/ar-rahman/1)

Notice the order. Before mentioning that He created human beings, God mentions that He taught the Quran. Commentators see this as a sign that guidance is the greatest of all blessings, even greater than life itself. The gift of speech, or "eloquence," comes next, as the ability to understand and share that guidance.

The rest of the surah unfolds that mercy step by step. It is a single long reflection on gratitude.

## Fabi ayyi ala i rabbikuma tukadhiban meaning

The refrain is the heart of the surah. In Arabic it reads *Fabi-ayyi ala'i rabbikuma tukadhdhiban*, and Sahih International translates it this way:

> So which of the favors of your Lord would you deny?
>
> — [Quran 55:13](/surah/ar-rahman/13)

Each word carries meaning:

- **Fa-bi-ayyi** means "so which" or "then which of."
- **Ala'i** means favors, blessings or wonders. Some commentators note it can also mean signs of God's power.
- **Rabbikuma** means "the Lord of you both." The ending *-kuma* is a dual form in Arabic, addressing two parties.
- **Tukadhdhiban** means "you two deny" or "you two call false."

The "two" being addressed are humans and jinn, unseen beings the Quran says were created from fire. The surah makes this clear when it describes both: "He created man from clay like [that of] pottery" and "He created the jinn from a smokeless flame of fire" ([Quran 55:14–15](/surah/ar-rahman/14)).

The question is not asking for information. It is a gentle challenge to the listener's heart. After every blessing, the reader is invited to pause and admit that none of it can honestly be denied.

### How did the jinn respond to the refrain?

A narration in Jami at-Tirmidhi reports that the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) recited Surah Ar-Rahman to his companions, who listened in silence. He told them that the jinn had responded better: each time he reached this verse, they said, "None of Your favors do we deny, our Lord, and to You belongs all praise." At-Tirmidhi described the narration as *gharib* (reported through a single chain), and later scholars such as al-Albani graded it hasan (good). Many Muslims repeat this response when they hear the refrain.

## What does Surah Ar-Rahman talk about?

The surah moves through four broad movements. Reading it with this map in mind makes its repetition feel purposeful rather than repetitive.

### 1. Blessings in creation (verses 1–30)

The opening verses point to the sun and moon moving "by precise calculation," the stars and trees bowing to God, and the earth laid out with fruit, palm trees and grain. A key passage introduces the idea of balance, or *mizan*.

> And the heaven He raised and imposed the balance That you not transgress within the balance. And establish weight in justice and do not make deficient the balance.
>
> — [Quran 55:7–9](/surah/ar-rahman/7)

The universe is held in balance, and people are told to keep balance and justice in their own dealings. The surah also describes two seas that meet with "a barrier [so] neither of them transgresses" ([Quran 55:19–20](/surah/ar-rahman/19)), and the pearls and coral that come from them.

### 2. Everything passes except God

At the center of the surah is a short, powerful reminder.

> Everyone upon the earth will perish, And there will remain the Face of your Lord, Owner of Majesty and Honor.
>
> — [Quran 55:26–27](/surah/ar-rahman/26)

Even this is followed by the refrain. The fact that this life ends, and that God remains, is itself presented as a favor, because it means justice and meaning do not end with death.

### 3. Judgment (verses 31–45)

The surah then turns to the Day of Judgment. It challenges jinn and humans to escape the bounds of the heavens and earth if they can, and describes the sky splitting open, "rose-colored like oil" ([Quran 55:37](/surah/ar-rahman/37)). Those who rejected the truth "will be known by their marks."

### 4. Two pairs of gardens (verses 46–78)

The final section describes Paradise in rich detail: flowing springs, fruit of every kind, and green cushions and fine carpets. It begins with a promise for those who were mindful of God.

> But for he who has feared the position of his Lord are two gardens -
>
> — [Quran 55:46](/surah/ar-rahman/46)

A second pair of gardens follows in [Quran 55:62](/surah/ar-rahman/62), which commentators understand as a slightly lower rank of Paradise. In the middle of this section is one of the most quoted lines of the Quran: "Is the reward for good [anything] but good?" ([Quran 55:60](/surah/ar-rahman/60)). The surah closes by blessing the name of God, "Owner of Majesty and Honor" ([Quran 55:78](/surah/ar-rahman/78)).

## Why is "which of the favours of your Lord would you deny" repeated 31 times?

Repetition is a feature of spoken Arabic eloquence, and Surah Ar-Rahman uses it with purpose. Each time the refrain appears, it follows a different blessing or scene, so it gathers new meaning as the surah goes on.

Scholars also explain that the refrain even follows descriptions of Hell. In those places, the "favor" is the warning itself: being told the truth in advance is a mercy, because it gives people the chance to change. When read aloud, the refrain gives the surah a rhythm that draws listeners in, which is one reason it is so loved.

## Surah Rahman benefits: what is authentic?

Many websites list benefits of Surah Rahman, from healing to marriage to wealth. It is important to separate authentic sources from popular claims.

- **Reward for reciting.** Every letter of the Quran recited brings reward. A hadith in Jami at-Tirmidhi, graded authentic, says each letter earns a good deed multiplied by ten.
- **Healing and mercy in general.** The Quran describes itself as "healing and mercy for the believers" ([Quran 17:82](/surah/al-isra/82)). This applies to the Quran as a whole, and many Muslims find recitation of Ar-Rahman calming and uplifting.
- **Gratitude.** The surah's clearest benefit is the one it teaches directly: noticing God's blessings and responding with thankfulness.
- **"The bride of the Quran."** A narration in al-Bayhaqi says "everything has a bride, and the bride of the Quran is Ar-Rahman." Scholars of hadith grade this narration as weak (da'if), so it should not be presented as a saying of the Prophet.

There is no authentic hadith that promises specific cures, marriages or wealth for reciting Surah Ar-Rahman a set number of times. Muslims may still recite it often and pray for what they need, as with any part of the Quran.

## Was Surah Ar-Rahman revealed in Mecca or Medina?

Scholars differ on this. Many classical commentators consider it a Meccan surah, and the story of the Prophet reciting it to the jinn supports that view. Some printed copies of the Quran label it Medinan, and Quran Universe follows that standard labeling. Our guide to [Meccan and Medinan surahs](/learn/meccan-and-medinan-surahs) explains how these labels are decided.

Ar-Rahman is followed directly by [Surah al-Waqiah](/learn/surah-al-waqiah), which also describes the Day of Judgment and the rewards of Paradise. Many Muslims read the two together.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does Surah Rahman mean?

"Ar-Rahman" means "The Most Merciful," one of the names of God in Islam. The surah is named after its first word and is a long reflection on God's mercy in creation, guidance and the life to come.

### What is the meaning of "fabi ayyi ala i rabbikuma tukadhiban"?

It means "So which of the favors of your Lord would you deny?" The Arabic is addressed to two parties, humans and jinn, and the verse is repeated 31 times in the surah after descriptions of God's blessings.

### How many verses are in Surah Ar-Rahman?

Surah Ar-Rahman has 78 verses. It is the 55th chapter of the Quran and is found in the 27th juz (part).

### What are the authentic benefits of Surah Rahman?

Like all of the Quran, reciting Surah Ar-Rahman earns reward for every letter, according to an authentic hadith in Jami at-Tirmidhi. There is no authentic hadith promising specific worldly results for this surah, and the popular saying that it is "the bride of the Quran" comes from a weak narration.

### Is Surah Rehman the same as Surah Ar-Rahman?

Yes. "Surah Rehman," "Surah Rahman" and "Surah A R Rahman" are different spellings of the same chapter, Surah 55. The spelling "Rehman" reflects how the name is often pronounced in Urdu and other South Asian languages.

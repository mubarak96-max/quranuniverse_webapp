Tawakkul means relying on God in Islam: doing everything within your power, then trusting Him completely with the result. It is not passive fatalism. The Prophet summed it up when a man asked whether to tie his camel or trust God: "Tie it and rely (upon Allah)." The Quran promises, "whoever relies upon Allah - then He is sufficient for him" (65:3).

Tawakkul is one of the qualities that most shapes how Muslims handle uncertainty, from job interviews to illness. This guide explains what tawakkul means, the key verses and hadith, how it differs from fatalism, and why many Muslims find it the most practical answer to anxiety.

## Key takeaways

- The Quran promises that "whoever relies upon Allah - then He is sufficient for him" ([Quran 65:3](/surah/at-talaq/3)).
- When a man asked whether to tie his camel or leave it and trust God, the Prophet said, "Tie it and rely (upon Allah)," according to Jami at-Tirmidhi 2517, graded hasan.
- In Jami at-Tirmidhi 2344, graded hasan, the Prophet said that true reliance on God would bring provision like a bird's, which "goes out in the morning empty, and returns full."
- The phrase *Hasbunallahu wa ni'mal wakil* comes from [Quran 3:173](/surah/ali-imran/173), and Sahih al-Bukhari 4563 reports that Prophet Ibrahim said it when thrown into the fire.
- The Quran tells the Prophet to consult people, decide, and then "rely upon Allah. Indeed, Allah loves those who rely [upon Him]" ([Quran 3:159](/surah/ali-imran/159)).

## What does tawakkul mean?

**Tawakkul** (*tawakkul*, توكل) comes from the Arabic root w-k-l, which means to entrust or delegate. A *wakil* is someone you appoint to handle your affairs. Tawakkul is the act of entrusting your affairs to God, after you have done your part, with confidence that He will handle them with wisdom.

One of God's names comes from the same root: [al-Wakil, the Trustee](/names/al-wakil), the one to whom affairs are entrusted. Another related name is [al-Hasib, the Sufficient](/names/al-hasib). To have tawakkul is to treat God as truly sufficient.

Scholars describe tawakkul as an action of the heart with two parts: genuine reliance on God, and taking the means He has made available. Remove the first and you have mere self-reliance. Remove the second and you have laziness dressed up as faith.

## What is the "tie your camel" hadith?

The best-known teaching on tawakkul is a short exchange reported by Anas ibn Malik:

> A man said: "O Messenger of Allah! Shall I tie it and rely (upon Allah), or leave it loose and rely (upon Allah)?" He said: "Tie it and rely (upon Allah)."

This hadith is in Jami at-Tirmidhi (2517) and is graded hasan (good). The popular English version, "Trust in God, but tie your camel," is a paraphrase of it.

The lesson is that effort and trust are not opposites. Tying the camel is the means. Trusting God is what the heart does while the hands work. The Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) lived this: he planned, consulted, wore armor in battle and hired a guide for his migration to Madinah, all while relying fully on God.

## What does the Quran say about trusting God?

Tawakkul appears throughout the Quran, often at the end of a command, as if to say: do this, then leave the rest to God.

### Quran 65:3: God is sufficient

> And will provide for him from where he does not expect. And whoever relies upon Allah - then He is sufficient for him. Indeed, Allah will accomplish His purpose. Allah has already set for everything a [decreed] extent.
>
> — [Quran 65:3](/surah/at-talaq/3)

This verse comes in a passage about divorce, one of the most stressful situations a person can face. It follows a promise that "whoever fears Allah - He will make for him a way out" ([Quran 65:2](/surah/at-talaq/2)). Tawakkul is offered precisely where people feel least in control.

### Quran 3:159: decide, then rely

> So by mercy from Allah, [O Muhammad], you were lenient with them. And if you had been rude [in speech] and harsh in heart, they would have disbanded from about you. So pardon them and ask forgiveness for them and consult them in the matter. And when you have decided, then rely upon Allah. Indeed, Allah loves those who rely [upon Him].
>
> — [Quran 3:159](/surah/ali-imran/159)

The order matters: consult, decide, then rely. Tawakkul comes after thinking and planning, not instead of them.

### Other verses on reliance

- Believers are those who, "upon their Lord they rely" ([Quran 8:2](/surah/al-anfal/2)).
- "Say, 'Never will we be struck except by what Allah has decreed for us; He is our protector.' And upon Allah let the believers rely" ([Quran 9:51](/surah/at-tawbah/51)).
- "And rely upon the Ever-Living who does not die" ([Quran 25:58](/surah/al-furqan/58)). People and plans fail; God does not.
- Prophet Yaqub advised his sons to enter Egypt through different gates as a precaution, then said, "upon Him I have relied, and upon Him let those who would rely [indeed] rely" ([Quran 12:67](/surah/yusuf/67)). Precaution and trust appear in the same verse.

## What is the bird hadith?

Umar ibn al-Khattab reported that the Prophet said:

> "If you were to rely upon Allah with the required reliance, then He would provide for you just as a bird is provided for, it goes out in the morning empty, and returns full."

This hadith is in Jami at-Tirmidhi (2344) and is graded hasan. Scholars point out a detail that is easy to miss: the bird *goes out*. It does not sit in its nest waiting for food to fall. The hadith praises trust that leaves home in the morning and does the work, while knowing that provision ultimately comes from [ar-Razzaq, the Provider](/names/ar-razzaq).

## What does Hasbunallahu wa ni'mal wakil mean?

*Hasbunallahu wa ni'mal wakil* (حَسۡبُنَا ٱللَّهُ وَنِعۡمَ ٱلۡوَكِيلُ) means "Sufficient for us is Allah, and [He is] the best Disposer of affairs." It comes from this verse:

> Those to whom hypocrites said, "Indeed, the people have gathered against you, so fear them." But it [merely] increased them in faith, and they said, "Sufficient for us is Allah, and [He is] the best Disposer of affairs."
>
> — [Quran 3:173](/surah/ali-imran/173)

Ibn Abbas reported that these words were said by Prophet Ibrahim (Abraham) when he was thrown into the fire, and by the Prophet Muhammad and his companions when they were told a great army had gathered against them (Sahih al-Bukhari 4563). The next verse records the outcome: "they returned with favor from Allah and bounty, no harm having touched them" ([Quran 3:174](/surah/ali-imran/174)).

Muslims say this phrase in moments of fear, injustice or overwhelming pressure. It is a declaration that God is enough, even when people are against you.

## Is tawakkul the same as fatalism?

No. Fatalism says the outcome is fixed, so effort is pointless. Tawakkul says the outcome is in God's hands, so do your best and stop being crushed by what you cannot control.

A famous incident shows the difference. When Umar ibn al-Khattab decided not to lead his army into a region struck by plague, Abu Ubaydah asked, "Are you running away from what Allah had ordained?" Umar replied, "Yes, we are running from what Allah had ordained to what Allah has ordained" (Sahih al-Bukhari 5729). Taking precautions is itself part of God's decree.

| Aspect | Tawakkul | Fatalism | Pure self-reliance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Effort | Full effort, using every lawful means | Little or none, since the result is fixed | Full effort |
| Where the heart rests | On God | On fate | On oneself |
| Response to failure | Accepts it as God's decree and tries again | Resignation | Self-blame or despair |
| Response to success | Gratitude to God | Indifference | Pride |
| Islamic view | Commanded and praised | Rejected | Incomplete |

The Prophet also said, "seek help from Allah and do not lose heart," and warned against saying "If only I had done that" after something goes wrong, because that "if" opens the door to Satan (Sahih Muslim 2664). In the same hadith he said a strong believer is better and more beloved to God than a weak one. Tawakkul makes a person stronger, not more passive.

Tawakkul is closely linked to belief in divine decree (*qadar*), one of the six articles of faith covered in [what Muslims believe](/learn/what-do-muslims-believe). It also explains why Muslims say [Inshallah](/learn/inshallah-meaning) about future plans: it is a statement of intent paired with trust.

## How does tawakkul help with anxiety?

Much anxiety comes from trying to control outcomes we cannot control. Tawakkul draws a line: your effort is your responsibility, and the result is God's. That division does not remove pain, but it removes the impossible burden of guaranteeing the future.

The Prophet taught a young Ibn Abbas: "Know that if the entire creation were to gather together to do something to benefit you, you would never get any benefit except that Allah had written for you. And if they were to gather to do something to harm you, you would never be harmed except that Allah had written for you" (Jami at-Tirmidhi 2516, graded hasan). Many Muslims find this freeing: no person holds ultimate power over their life.

Tawakkul does not replace professional help for anxiety disorders. Seeking treatment is itself "tying the camel." For verses and prophetic prayers for difficult moments, see our guide to [dua for anxiety and stress](/learn/dua-for-anxiety-and-stress). For decisions where you are unsure which path to take, Islam offers the [istikhara prayer](/learn/istikhara-prayer), which is tawakkul put into practice.

## How to practice tawakkul daily

1. **Begin with the leaving-home dua.** "In the name of Allah, I trust in Allah; there is no might and no power but in Allah." The Prophet said whoever says it is told, "You are guided, defended and protected" (Sunan Abi Dawud 5095, graded sahih).
2. **Plan seriously, then let go.** After preparing for an exam, interview or surgery, consciously hand the result to God.
3. **Say Hasbunallah when afraid.** Use the words of Ibrahim and the companions in moments of fear.
4. **Watch your "if onlys."** Replace regret with "Allah decreed, and He does what He wills."
5. **Pair it with patience.** When the outcome is not what you hoped, tawakkul continues as [sabr, patience](/learn/sabr-patience-in-islam).

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the meaning of tawakkul?

Tawakkul means reliance or trust in God. It comes from the Arabic root w-k-l, meaning to entrust. In Islam, it means doing everything within your ability and then relying on God for the outcome, confident in His wisdom. The Quran says, "whoever relies upon Allah - then He is sufficient for him" (65:3).

### What is the tie your camel hadith?

A man asked the Prophet whether he should tie his camel and trust God, or leave it untied and trust God. The Prophet replied, "Tie it and rely (upon Allah)." It is recorded in Jami at-Tirmidhi 2517 and graded hasan. It teaches that effort and trust in God go together.

### What does Hasbunallahu wa ni'mal wakil mean?

It means "Sufficient for us is Allah, and He is the best Disposer of affairs." The phrase comes from Quran 3:173. According to Sahih al-Bukhari 4563, Prophet Ibrahim said it when thrown into the fire, and the Prophet Muhammad and his companions said it when warned that an army had gathered against them.

### Is tawakkul the same as doing nothing?

No. Islam rejects passive fatalism. The Prophet told a man to tie his camel and then rely on God, and Umar avoided a plague-stricken land, calling it moving "from what Allah had ordained to what Allah has ordained." Tawakkul means full effort with the heart resting on God, not on one's own plans.

### What are some Quran verses about trust in Allah?

Key verses include 65:3 ("whoever relies upon Allah - then He is sufficient for him"), 3:159 ("when you have decided, then rely upon Allah"), 3:173 ("Sufficient for us is Allah"), 9:51 ("upon Allah let the believers rely") and 25:58 ("rely upon the Ever-Living who does not die").

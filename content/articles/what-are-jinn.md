Jinn are beings that, according to the Quran, God created from "a smokeless flame of fire" before He created humans. They live alongside people but are normally invisible. Like humans, they have free will, can believe or disbelieve, and will be judged by God. Some jinn are righteous Muslims, while the devils (shayatin) are the jinn who follow Iblis.

The word "jinn" is often confused with the genies of fairy tales or with Western ideas of demons. Islam's picture is different from both. This guide explains what the Quran and authentic hadith teach about jinn, what Surah al-Jinn says, how jinn differ from angels and demons, how Muslims seek protection, and how to think about claims of possession in a balanced way.

## Key takeaways

- The Quran says God "created the jinn from a smokeless flame of fire" ([Quran 55:15](/surah/ar-rahman/15)), and Sahih Muslim 2996 states that angels were created from light, jinn from fire and Adam from clay.
- Jinn were created to worship God, just as humans were ([Quran 51:56](/surah/adh-dhariyat/56)), and they will be held accountable on the Day of Judgment ([Quran 6:130](/surah/al-anam/130)).
- Iblis, the devil, "was of the jinn" and not an angel ([Quran 18:50](/surah/al-kahf/50)).
- Surah al-Jinn, the 72nd chapter of the Quran, records a group of jinn hearing the Quran and accepting Islam ([Quran 72:1-2](/surah/al-jinn/1)).
- Sahih al-Bukhari 2311 teaches that reciting Ayat al-Kursi before sleep brings protection from devils until morning.

## What are jinn in Islam?

**Jinn** (*jinn*, جن) are a separate creation from both humans and angels. The Arabic root j-n-n means to hide or conceal, which reflects their main quality: they exist, but people normally cannot see them. A single jinn is called a *jinni*.

The Quran describes the material they were made from in two places. One says they were created "from a smokeless flame of fire" ([Quran 55:15](/surah/ar-rahman/15)). The other says they were made "before" humans "from scorching fire" ([Quran 15:27](/surah/al-hijr/27)). A hadith narrated by Aisha in Sahih Muslim 2996 sums up the three kinds of beings: angels were created from light, jinn from smokeless fire, and Adam from what the Quran has described, meaning clay.

### Are jinn real?

For Muslims, belief in jinn is part of faith, because the Quran mentions them many times and devotes a whole surah to them. Belief in the unseen (*al-ghayb*) is a basic feature of Islam, and the jinn are one part of that unseen world.

Belief in jinn is widespread in Muslim societies, though it varies by country. In a 2012 survey across 23 countries, the [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/religion/2012/08/09/the-worlds-muslims-unity-and-diversity-4-other-beliefs-and-practices/) found that a majority of Muslims in 13 of them said jinn exist, with figures as high as 84% in Bangladesh and 86% in Morocco.

## Do jinn have free will?

Yes. This is what sets jinn apart from angels in Islamic belief. Angels obey God without exception, while jinn, like humans, can choose. The Quran addresses both together as the beings with responsibility:

> And I did not create the jinn and mankind except to worship Me.
>
> — [Quran 51:56](/surah/adh-dhariyat/56)

Because they can choose, jinn are divided into believers and disbelievers, the righteous and the wrongdoers. The Quran quotes the jinn themselves saying: "among us are the righteous, and among us are [others] not so; we were [of] divided ways" ([Quran 72:11](/surah/al-jinn/11)). On the Day of Judgment, God will address "the company of jinn and mankind" and ask whether messengers came to them ([Quran 6:130](/surah/al-anam/130)).

## Who is Iblis?

**Iblis** is the jinn who refused God's command to bow to Adam. Many people assume he was a fallen angel, but the Quran says plainly that he was a jinn:

> And [mention] when We said to the angels, "Prostrate to Adam," and they prostrated, except for Iblees. He was of the jinn and departed from the command of his Lord. Then will you take him and his descendants as allies other than Me while they are enemies to you? Wretched it is for the wrongdoers as an exchange.
>
> — [Quran 18:50](/surah/al-kahf/50)

His reason was pride. He said, "I am better than him. You created me from fire and created him from clay" ([Quran 7:12](/surah/al-araf/12)). From then on, he became **Shaytan**, the devil, and vowed to mislead humanity. ## What does Surah al-Jinn say?

[Surah al-Jinn](/surah/al-jinn) is the 72nd surah of the Quran, a Makkan chapter of 28 verses. It opens with a group of jinn who overheard the Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) reciting the Quran:

> Say, [O Muhammad], "It has been revealed to me that a group of the jinn listened and said, 'Indeed, we have heard an amazing Qur'an.
>
> — [Quran 72:1](/surah/al-jinn/1)

The jinn then describe their own beliefs: they reject the idea that God has a wife or son, admit that some of them used to say false things about God, and note that some humans used to seek refuge with jinn, which only "increased them in burden" ([Quran 72:6](/surah/al-jinn/6)). They also report that they used to eavesdrop on the heavens but are now driven off by flames ([Quran 72:9](/surah/al-jinn/9)).

The surah's lesson is that the Quran's message is for jinn as well as humans, and that no jinn or human can know the unseen or share in God's authority. A similar account appears in [Quran 46:29-32](/surah/al-ahqaf/29), where jinn hear the Quran and return to their people as warners. For more on the Quran's chapters, see [how many surahs and verses are in the Quran](/learn/how-many-surahs-and-verses-in-the-quran).

## What do jinn look like?

The Quran does not describe the natural appearance of jinn. It says they see humans "from where you do not see them" ([Quran 7:27](/surah/al-araf/27)). Authentic hadith show they can sometimes take visible forms. In Sahih al-Bukhari 2311, a devil appeared to the companion Abu Hurayrah as a man stealing from charity food. In Sahih al-Bukhari 461, the Prophet said an *'ifrit*, a powerful jinn, came to disturb his prayer.

The Quran also describes jinn who worked for the Prophet Solomon by God's permission, building for him ([Quran 34:12-13](/surah/saba/12)). Yet when Solomon died leaning on his staff, the jinn did not notice until a creature ate through it, proving they did not know the unseen ([Quran 34:14](/surah/saba/14)). Popular images of jinn as smoke-like giants come largely from folklore, not from the Quran.

## Jinn vs demons vs genies: what is the difference?

The English words used for jinn carry ideas from other traditions, which can be misleading.

| Term | Origin | Nature | Free will? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Jinn (Islam) | Quran and hadith | Created from fire, unseen, morally mixed | Yes, believers and disbelievers |
| Shayatin (devils) | Quran and hadith | The disbelieving jinn who follow Iblis | Yes, and they chose evil |
| Demons (Christian tradition) | Christian theology | Generally understood as fallen angels | Rebelled against God |
| Genie (Western folklore) | Arabian Nights translations | Wish-granting spirit, often in a lamp | Varies by story |
| Angels (Islam) | Quran and hadith | Created from light, always obedient | No, they never disobey |

The English word "genie" comes from French *génie*, from Latin *genius*. According to [Etymonline](https://www.etymonline.com/word/genie), French translators of the *Arabian Nights* in the 18th century used it for the Arabic *jinni* because the words sounded alike, though the two are unrelated. The magic lamp and three wishes belong to storytelling, not to Islamic belief. In Islam, jinn cannot grant wishes, and seeking their help is condemned.

For how the Quran describes angels, the other unseen creation, see our guide to [angels in Islam](/learn/angels-in-islam).

## What are jinn scared of? Protection in Islam

Muslims do not believe jinn should be feared above God. The Quran teaches that the devil's power over believers is limited to whispering and suggestion, and that the response is to seek refuge in God ([Quran 41:36](/surah/fussilat/36)). Authentic sources point to specific protections.

- **Ayat al-Kursi.** In Sahih al-Bukhari 2311, the Prophet confirmed that whoever recites [Ayat al-Kursi](/surah/al-baqarah/255) at bedtime has a guardian from God, and no devil comes near until morning. Our guide to [Ayatul Kursi](/learn/ayatul-kursi) explains the verse in full.
- **The last three surahs.** Surah al-Ikhlas, Surah al-Falaq and Surah an-Nas are recited for protection, and the last ends by seeking refuge from whisperers "among the jinn and mankind" ([Quran 114:6](/surah/an-nas/6)). See our guide to the [four Quls](/learn/four-quls).
- **Surah al-Baqarah at home.** Sahih Muslim 780 records that Satan flees from a house in which Surah al-Baqarah is recited.
- **The call to prayer.** Sahih al-Bukhari 608 says Satan runs away when the adhan is called.
- **Saying God's name.** Sahih al-Bukhari 3280 advises closing doors at night while mentioning God's name, because devils do not open a closed door.

Sahih Muslim 2814 adds that every person has a jinn companion (*qarin*) assigned to them, which is why the Prophet taught constant remembrance of God rather than anxiety about the unseen.

## Jinn possession in Islam: a balanced view

Claims of jinn possession are common in some communities, and this is where care matters most.

### What the scholars say

Most classical Sunni scholars held that jinn can affect a person physically, citing [Quran 2:275](/surah/al-baqarah/275), which compares those who consume usury to "one who is being beaten by Satan into insanity." Ibn Taymiyyah, among others, affirmed that possession can happen. The Mu'tazili theologians denied it, and some modern scholars read the verse figuratively and are skeptical of most possession claims. Muslims who accept that possession is possible still consider it rare.

### Medical care comes first

Many symptoms attributed to jinn, such as seizures, hearing voices, sudden changes in behavior, depression or anxiety, are also signs of medical and psychiatric conditions that respond to treatment. Islam itself instructs people to seek medicine. In Sunan Abi Dawud 3855, graded sahih, the Prophet said: "Make use of medical treatment, for Allah has not made a disease without appointing a remedy for it." Sahih al-Bukhari 5678 records a similar teaching.

Anyone worried about themselves or a family member should see a doctor or mental health professional first. Prayer and **ruqyah**, the recitation of Quran and authentic supplications for healing, can be used alongside medical care, never instead of it. Sahih Muslim 2200 permits ruqyah as long as it contains nothing of associating partners with God.

### Warning signs of abuse

Scholars and doctors warn against so-called exorcists who charge large fees, use amulets or unknown words, isolate the patient, or use beating and force. These practices have caused serious harm to vulnerable people. Authentic ruqyah is simple: reciting the Quran, praying for the sick person and trusting God. Islam forbids seeking help from jinn or fortune-tellers in any form.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is jinn in Islam?

In Islam, jinn are beings created by God from smokeless fire, as stated in Quran 55:15. They are normally invisible to humans, live in the same world, eat and have families, and have free will. Like humans, they are commanded to worship God and will be judged. Some are believers, and the disbelievers among them are called devils.

### Are jinn real in Islam?

Yes. Belief in jinn is part of Islamic faith because the Quran mentions them dozens of times and devotes Surah al-Jinn, the 72nd chapter, to them. Muslims see jinn as part of the unseen world that God created. A 2012 Pew Research Center survey found a majority of Muslims in 13 of 23 countries said jinn exist.

### What are jinn scared of?

Islamic sources say devils flee from the remembrance of God. Sahih al-Bukhari 2311 says Ayat al-Kursi protects a person at night, Sahih Muslim 780 says Satan flees a house where Surah al-Baqarah is recited, and Sahih al-Bukhari 608 says he runs from the call to prayer. Muslims also recite the last three surahs of the Quran for protection.

### What is the difference between jinn and demons?

In Islam, jinn are a whole species with free will, some good and some evil. The evil ones who follow Iblis are called shayatin, or devils, which is closest to the English "demons." In Christian tradition, demons are usually understood as fallen angels, but Islam teaches that angels never disobey and that Iblis was a jinn (Quran 18:50).

### Is Iblis a jinn or an angel?

Iblis is a jinn. Quran 18:50 states that "he was of the jinn and departed from the command of his Lord." He was present among the angels when God commanded them to bow to Adam, but he refused out of pride, saying he was made of fire and Adam of clay (Quran 7:12).

### Can jinn possess humans?

Most classical Sunni scholars held that possession is possible, citing Quran 2:275, while the Mu'tazila and some modern scholars disagreed. All agree it should not be assumed. Symptoms often blamed on jinn can be medical or psychiatric, so a doctor should be seen first, as the Prophet encouraged in Sunan Abi Dawud 3855. Ruqyah can accompany treatment.

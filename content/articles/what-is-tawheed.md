Tawheed is the Islamic belief that God is absolutely one: one Creator and Lord of everything, the only one worthy of worship, and unique in His names and attributes, with nothing like Him. It is the first and most important teaching of Islam, summed up in the declaration "there is no god but Allah," and every other belief and practice rests on it.

Its opposite is *shirk*, associating partners with God, which the Quran calls the one sin God will not forgive if a person dies upon it. This guide explains what tawheed means, how scholars break it into categories, what counts as shirk, and how the belief shapes everyday Muslim life.

## Key takeaways

- **Tawheed** comes from the Arabic root w-h-d, meaning "one," and literally means "making one" or affirming oneness.
- Surah al-Ikhlas ([Quran 112](/surah/al-ikhlas)) is the classic summary of tawheed, and the Prophet said it equals a third of the Quran (Sahih al-Bukhari 5013).
- The Quran teaches that every messenger brought the same core message: "There is no deity except Me, so worship Me" ([Quran 21:25](/surah/al-anbya/25)).
- Many scholars teach tawheed in three categories: lordship (*rububiyyah*), worship (*uluhiyyah*), and names and attributes (*al-asma' wa al-sifat*). This is a teaching framework, and other schools of theology organize the same belief differently.
- The Quran states that God "does not forgive association with Him, but He forgives what is less than that for whom He wills" ([Quran 4:48](/surah/an-nisa/48)).
- The Prophet called showing off in worship "minor shirk," in a hadith in Musnad Ahmad graded *hasan*.

## What does tawheed mean?

**Tawheed** (*tawhid*, توحيد) is a verbal noun from the Arabic root w-h-d, the same root as *wahid* (one) and *ahad* (the One). Linguistically it means declaring something to be one or treating it as one. In Islamic belief it means affirming that God is one in His being, His actions, His right to be worshipped and His attributes.

Both spellings, "tawheed" and "tawhid," are common in English. They are transliterations of the same Arabic word.

Tawheed is not only a statement about how many gods exist. It is a claim about where a person directs their worship, trust, love, hope and fear. The Prophet Muhammad (peace be upon him) asked his companion Mu'adh ibn Jabal whether he knew God's right over His servants, and then answered: that they worship Him and associate nothing with Him (Sahih al-Bukhari 2856).

## The Quranic evidence for tawheed

The whole Quran returns to tawheed again and again, but three passages are the anchors scholars cite most.

### Surah al-Ikhlas

> Say, "He is Allah, [who is] One, Allah, the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor is born, Nor is there to Him any equivalent."
>
> — [Quran 112:1–4](/surah/al-ikhlas/1)

In four lines, the surah affirms God's oneness, His perfect self-sufficiency, and His freedom from having parents, children or equals. Its name, *al-Ikhlas*, means "sincerity" or "purity," because it purifies belief about God. Muslims recite it daily, as explained in our guide to the [four Quls](/learn/four-quls).

### One God, the Merciful

> And your god is one God. There is no deity [worthy of worship] except Him, the Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful.
>
> — [Quran 2:163](/surah/al-baqarah/163)

This verse joins oneness to mercy. God's oneness is not described as cold or distant; the same verse names Him [Ar-Rahman](/names/ar-rahman) and [Ar-Raheem](/names/ar-raheem).

### The argument from order

> Had there been within the heavens and earth gods besides Allah, they both would have been ruined. So exalted is Allah, Lord of the Throne, above what they describe.
>
> — [Quran 21:22](/surah/al-anbya/22)

Here the Quran offers a reasoned argument: multiple gods with independent wills would produce conflict and disorder, yet the universe holds together under one consistent order. A similar argument appears in [Quran 23:91](/surah/al-muminun/91).

## What are the types of tawheed?

Many scholars, particularly following the Hanbali theologian Ibn Taymiyyah and those influenced by him, teach tawheed in three categories. The division is a way of organizing the Quran's teaching for students, not a revealed list, and it is widely used in Islamic schools and courses today.

### Tawheed ar-rububiyyah: oneness of lordship

This is the belief that God alone creates, owns, sustains and controls everything. Nothing happens in the universe except by His will. The Quran notes that even the idol worshippers of Makkah accepted this much: "If you asked them, 'Who created the heavens and earth and subjected the sun and the moon?' they would surely say, 'Allah'" ([Quran 29:61](/surah/al-ankabut/61)). For this reason scholars stress that belief in a Creator alone is not enough.

### Tawheed al-uluhiyyah: oneness of worship

This is the belief that God alone deserves worship, so every act of devotion, from prayer and sacrifice to dua and vows, is directed only to Him. Muslims affirm it in every unit of prayer when reciting Surah al-Fatihah: "It is You we worship and You we ask for help" ([Quran 1:5](/surah/al-fatihah/5)). The Quran presents this as the message of every prophet, as [Quran 16:36](/surah/an-nahl/36) says: "Worship Allah and avoid Taghut," meaning false objects of worship.

### Tawheed al-asma' wa al-sifat: oneness of names and attributes

This is the belief that God's names and attributes are unique and perfect, and that nothing in creation shares them in the way He has them. The key verse is: "There is nothing like unto Him, and He is the Hearing, the Seeing" ([Quran 42:11](/surah/ash-shuraa/11)). Believers affirm what God says about Himself, without comparing Him to creation and without emptying His attributes of meaning. Our [99 Names of Allah](/names) pages explore these names one by one.

### Other ways of explaining tawheed

Theologians of the Ash'ari and Maturidi schools, which many Sunni Muslims follow, usually explain tawheed differently: God is one in His essence (*dhat*), one in His attributes (*sifat*) and one in His actions (*af'al*). The two frameworks agree on the core: one God, alone deserving worship, with no equal. They differ mainly in emphasis and in some questions about how to understand God's attributes.

| Category | Arabic | What it affirms | Key verse |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lordship | Tawheed ar-rububiyyah | God alone creates, owns and controls all things | [Quran 29:61](/surah/al-ankabut/61) |
| Worship | Tawheed al-uluhiyyah | God alone deserves every act of worship | [Quran 1:5](/surah/al-fatihah/5) |
| Names and attributes | Tawheed al-asma' wa al-sifat | God's names and attributes are perfect and unlike any creature's | [Quran 42:11](/surah/ash-shuraa/11) |

## What is shirk?

**Shirk** (*shirk*, شرك) means associating partners with God: giving something else a share of what belongs to Him alone, whether in lordship, worship or attributes. The word comes from a root meaning "to share" or "to partner." The Quran treats it as the gravest wrong a person can commit.

> And [mention, O Muhammad], when Luqman said to his son while he was instructing him, "O my son, do not associate [anything] with Allah. Indeed, association [with him] is great injustice."
>
> — [Quran 31:13](/surah/luqman/13)

The Quran also says God "does not forgive association with Him, but He forgives what is less than that for whom He wills" ([Quran 4:48](/surah/an-nisa/48)). Scholars explain that this refers to a person who dies upon shirk without repenting. Anyone who repents sincerely, from any sin including shirk, is forgiven, as [Quran 39:53](/surah/az-zumar/53) promises.

### Major shirk

Major shirk (*shirk akbar*) means directing worship to anyone or anything other than God, such as praying to idols, saints, the dead or any created being, or believing that something besides God independently controls the universe. It contradicts tawheed at its root, and scholars agree it takes a person outside Islam.

### Minor shirk

Minor shirk (*shirk asghar*) does not remove a person from Islam but is still a serious sin. The best-known example is **riya'**, showing off in worship. The Prophet said that what he feared most for his community was "minor shirk," and when asked what it was, he said: showing off (Musnad Ahmad 23630, graded *hasan*).

Another example is swearing by something other than God. The Prophet said, "Whoever swears by other than Allah has committed disbelief or shirk" (Jami' at-Tirmidhi 1535, graded *hasan* by at-Tirmidhi). Commentators explain that this wording is a strong warning, and it becomes major shirk only if the person gives the thing sworn by the reverence due to God.

| Aspect | Major shirk | Minor shirk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Definition | Directing worship to other than God, or ascribing His powers to others | Acts that compromise sincerity without worshipping another |
| Examples | Praying to idols or the dead; believing a creature controls fate independently | Showing off in worship; swearing by other than God |
| Effect | Contradicts tawheed and takes a person out of Islam | A serious sin that can cancel the reward of the deed it affects; does not remove a person from Islam |
| Remedy | Sincere repentance and returning to tawheed | Repentance and correcting one's intention |

## Tawheed in daily life

For Muslims, tawheed is not an abstract doctrine. It shapes how a person feels and acts throughout the day.

- **Trust.** Because God alone controls outcomes, a believer works hard and then relies on Him, a quality called *tawakkul*. "Whoever relies upon Allah - then He is sufficient for him" ([Quran 65:3](/surah/at-talaq/3)). See [tawakkul: trusting God in Islam](/learn/tawakkul-trust-in-god).
- **Fear and hope.** A Muslim fears displeasing God more than displeasing people, and hopes in God more than in any person, job or bank balance. Tawheed frees a person from being controlled by the opinions of others.
- **Sincerity in worship.** Prayer, fasting and charity are done for God alone, not for praise. "Indeed, my prayer, my rites of sacrifice, my living and my dying are for Allah, Lord of the worlds" ([Quran 6:162](/surah/al-anam/162)).
- **Dignity and equality.** If only God deserves worship, no human being can claim it. Every person stands before God as His servant, equal in that respect.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the meaning of tawhid?

Tawhid, also spelled tawheed, means affirming the oneness of God. It comes from the Arabic root w-h-d, meaning "one." In Islam it means believing that God alone creates and controls everything, that He alone deserves worship, and that His names and attributes are unique, with nothing like Him (Quran 42:11).

### What are the three types of tawheed?

Many scholars teach tawheed as three categories: *rububiyyah* (God alone is Lord and Creator), *uluhiyyah* (God alone deserves worship) and *al-asma' wa al-sifat* (God's names and attributes are unique). The framework is a teaching aid associated with Ibn Taymiyyah. Ash'ari and Maturidi theologians describe God's oneness in essence, attributes and actions instead.

### What is shirk in Islam?

Shirk means associating partners with God by giving anything else a share in His lordship, His worship or His attributes. Major shirk, such as worshipping idols or the dead, contradicts Islam. Minor shirk includes showing off in worship. The Quran calls shirk "great injustice" (31:13), and repentance from it is always open.

### Is shirk an unforgivable sin?

The Quran says God does not forgive shirk but forgives lesser sins for whom He wills (4:48). Scholars explain this applies to someone who dies while committing shirk without repenting. A person who sincerely repents during their lifetime is forgiven, since God "forgives all sins" for those who turn back to Him (39:53).

### What is the oneness of God in Islam?

The oneness of God in Islam, called tawheed, means God is a single, undivided being with no partner, child or equal. He alone created the universe, He alone deserves worship, and no creature shares His perfect attributes. Surah al-Ikhlas summarizes it: "He is Allah, One... nor is there to Him any equivalent."
